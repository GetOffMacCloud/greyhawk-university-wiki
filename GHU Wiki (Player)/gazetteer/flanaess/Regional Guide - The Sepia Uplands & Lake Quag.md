# 🗺️ Regional Guide: The Sepia Uplands & Lake Quag

> [!quote] Perrenlander proverb
> *"The Sepia does not kill you with a single blow. It takes everything slowly — your warmth, your path, your name — until you are just another stone on the moor."*

---

## I. Overview

The **Sepia Uplands** are a vast region of high moorland, rolling stone hills, and fog-choked valleys stretching north of the **Clatspur Mountains**, between the cold shores of **Lake Quag** to the northwest and the tree-line of the **Vesve Forest** to the east. Politically, the region exists in a grey zone — technically within the sphere of **Perrenland's** influence, yet almost entirely ungoverned. The Concatenated Cantons claim portions of it on paper, but their soldiers rarely march past the town of **Traft** and their tax-collectors never venture here at all.

In 576 CY, the Sepia Uplands are a true frontier: a no-man's land populated by miners, trappers, nomads, dwarven clanholds, gnome prospectors, druidic enclaves, and the scattered remnants of older peoples who remember when these hills had a different name. The founding of **Ritterstadt** at the uplands' northern edge — where moor gives way to Quag Mire — marks one of the most remote inhabited outposts on the continent.

## II. The Land Itself — Terrain Types

The Sepia Uplands are named for the color they take on in late autumn and winter: a tawny, amber-brown expanse of dead heather, ochre grass, and rust-colored bracken stretching to the horizon under a grey sky. In summer the color softens to pale greens and muted golds, but the landscape never fully loses its sepia character.

### 🟤 The High Moor (Central Uplands)
The heart of the region — rolling waves of open moorland at elevations of 1,200 to 2,400 feet. The ground is spongy with peat, treacherous in wet seasons when it becomes a quaking surface over deep bog holes. Visibility is excellent on clear days, but the uplands generate their own weather: fog banks can roll in within minutes, reducing sight lines to twenty feet. Heather, sedge grass, and low gorse dominate the vegetation. Stone outcroppings break the surface like the knuckles of a buried giant.

**Travel conditions:** Slow going off-path. The peat holds moisture and softens after rain. Horses tire quickly; wagons cannot cross without established tracks.
**Key phrase:** *"Amber heather to the horizon, and grey stone knuckles breaking the peat."*

### 🏔️ The Ridge Lines (Western & Northern Sepia)
A series of parallel stone ridges run roughly north-south through the western uplands, their crests bare rock scoured clean by centuries of wind. These ridges — locally called **the Teeth** — rise to 3,000–4,000 feet and define natural corridors that funnel travel. The passes between the Teeth are the only reliable wagon routes. Dwarf strongholds occupy the most defensible of these crests.

**Travel conditions:** Exposed and brutal in winter. The ridges generate downdrafts that make camping on the heights dangerous. The valleys between them are protected and often hold small tarns (mountain lakes) fed by snowmelt.
**Key phrase:** *"Bare grey crests in a marching row, and the wind never stops talking through the gaps."*

### 🌿 The River Valleys (Eastern Sepia)
The eastern Sepia drains into the Vesve watershed. Rivers cut deep, forested valleys into the upland plateau — the walls steep enough to feel like canyons after the open moor. These valleys are sheltered from wind, significantly warmer in summer, and heavily wooded with birch, ash, and older growths of pine and yew. Gnome settlements cluster in these valleys near mineral seams; the occasional halfling community occupies the wider, flatter stretches near the valley floors.

**Travel conditions:** Excellent shelter but maze-like. The valleys frequently split without warning; getting lost in the eastern valley complex is a genuine danger. Gnome guides are invaluable.
**Key phrase:** *"Steep green walls, the smell of old yew, and the sky narrowed to a ribbon overhead."*

### 🌫️ The Margin Bogs (Northern Edge)
Where the Sepia Uplands approach the Quag Mires, the terrain transitions through a band of semi-stable bog: solid enough to walk on in summer, treacherous in spring thaw and autumn freeze. The ground here is stained dark with tannins, the water in the bog holes tea-black and ice-cold even in July. Twisted alder thickets and dead standing timber mark the most waterlogged areas. This is Ritterstadt's immediate hinterland — the zone of primary exploration for University expeditions.

**Travel conditions:** Treat as difficult terrain throughout. Thin ice over bog holes is a persistent hazard from Patchwall through Coldeven. Guides and poles are standard equipment. In the current season (Planting), the spring thaw has made the margin bogs especially unstable.
**Key phrase:** *"Tea-black water, dead standing timber, and ground that breathes under your boots."*

### 🏔️ The Clatspur Foothills (Southern Approach)
The southern Sepia rises into the arms of the Clatspur Mountains, which separate the uplands from Perrenland proper. These foothills are heavily forested with spruce and fir, crosscut by fast-moving streams draining off the peaks. The town of **Traft** sits at the foot of the main pass — the last reliable market town before you climb into Sepia proper.
**Key phrase:** *"Spruce-dark slopes, cold fast water, and the mountains leaning over your shoulder."*

## III. Climate — Season by Season

The Sepia Uplands operate under a subarctic upland climate: short, mild summers; long, punishing winters; and shoulder seasons defined by rapid, dangerous transitions.

### ❄️ Needfest & Fireseek — *The Deep Winter* (Midwinter – Early Spring)
The Sepia in winter is a different world. The moor goes white and silent. Wind screams across the ridges, dropping effective temperatures to −20°F and below during the worst storms. Rivers freeze hard enough to walk on. The margin bogs lock solid under two feet of ice — paradoxically making northern travel easier than in other seasons, if you can survive the cold. Snowdrifts fill the valley passes; the Teeth become impassable to all but dwarves.

- **Hazards:** Extreme cold (1 level of exhaustion per hour without shelter in storms), blizzard visibility, avalanche risk on the ridges, frozen marsh travel (deceptively fast but hollow-ice death traps)
- **Day length:** 8–9 hours of grey light. True dark by late afternoon.
- **Creatures active:** Wolves, winter wolves, frost giant patrols (rare), undead stirred by the cold, polar bear-kin from the far north

### 🌱 Readying & Coldeven — *The Cruel Spring* (Spring)
The most treacherous season. Snow melts but temperatures remain bitter; the moor becomes a brown slurry; the bog margins turn to black water. Rivers flood. The Quag Mire "bleeds" into the northern Sepia as meltwater overwhelms drainage. Fog is near-constant in the lowlands. Travel is miserable and dangerous.

- **Hazards:** Flash flooding in valleys, collapse of bog ice, white-out fog events (1d4 hours, no navigation), hypothermia from wet cold
- **Day length:** Lengthening to 12+ hours, but overcast
- **Creatures active:** Awakening predators (lean and hungry), territorial nesting raptors, the first Wolf Nomad outriders

### 🌿 Growfest & Planting — *The Bitter Green* (Early Summer) ← **CURRENT SEASON**
The uplands *technically* bloom in Planting. Heather puts out pale purple flowers; bog cotton drifts white across the moss; the valleys turn vivid green. But summer here is relative. Frosts persist well into Planting. Nights are cold. The wind off Lake Quag carries a killing edge even on bright days. In a normal year, Planting is the most beautiful month on the Sepia. In 576 CY, the **Blackfrost Bloom** has delayed true spring by three to four weeks, leaving temperatures closer to Coldeven levels.

- **Hazards:** Night frost (1d4 cold damage to unprepared sleepers), unstable bog margins, sudden fog from Lake Quag, Nomad migration routes cutting through
- **Day length:** 15+ hours; long twilight. True dark is brief.
- **Creatures active:** Most creatures are active. This is peak season for raptors, bog predators, giant insects emerging from the marsh, and the first University expeditions out of Ritterstadt.

### ☀️ Flocktime & Wealsun — *The Short Summer* (Summer)
The window. Six to eight weeks where the Sepia is genuinely hospitable — relatively speaking. Temperatures reach 55–65°F on good days. The high moor is accessible and beautiful. Rivers run clear. Visibility from the ridges is exceptional. This is the prime expedition season and the only time caravans reliably make the full circuit between Traft, the interior settlements, and Lake Quag's shore.

- **Hazards:** Summer storms roll off Lake Quag with little warning, bringing lightning and hail. Bog insects (particularly the giant Sepia horsefly and the black marsh midge) are at peak nuisance. Nomad presence is heaviest.
- **Day length:** 16+ hours. Genuine midnight sun effect near the solstice.
- **Creatures active:** Everything. Peak season for giants, dragons reconnoitering from the Clatspurs, gnoll packs from the east, travelers, merchants.

### 🍂 Richfest through Brewfest — *The Amber Dying* (Late Summer / Harvest)
The Sepia shows its colors in this season — the heather browns, the bracken turns copper, the grasses go gold. It is genuinely beautiful. It is also the season of preparation for what comes next. Miners pull out, trappers move to winter camps, the last caravans run before the passes close. A melancholy urgency defines Sepia autumn.

- **Hazards:** First early frosts by Ready'reat. Storms increasing in frequency. Nomads at peak movement, heading south before winter.
- **Day length:** Shortening rapidly.
- **Creatures active:** Migration patterns create unusual encounters. Animals fattening for winter are aggressive. Undead from the Quag Mire become more active as cold returns.

### 🌨️ Patchwall & Ready'reat — *The Hard Shoulder* (Late Autumn / Early Winter)
The killing zone between the amber season and true winter. Snow on the ridges by Patchwall; permanent snow cover on the moor by Ready'reat. The passes close. Anyone still in the deep uplands either has a winter camp prepared or is in serious trouble. Ritterstadt battens down, adds a third watch, and doesn't open the palisade gate after dark.

## IV. Flora

The plant life of the Sepia Uplands is hardy, low-growing, and specialized. Nothing tall survives on the open moor; the wind sees to that.

### Common Plants
| Plant | Appearance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sepia Heather** | Knee-high purple-pink flowering shrub | Blooms Planting–Flocktime. Dried stalks burn well. Distilled into a bitter tea for warmth. |
| **Bog Cotton** | White tufted seedheads on thin stems | Marks stable bog vs. quaking ground. Where cotton grows thickly, the ground holds. Where it thins: test before you step. |
| **Clatspur Gorse** | Dense yellow-flowered thorned shrub | Impenetrable thickets. Excellent windbreak and animal cover. |
| **Black Sedge** | Dark wiry grass in clumps | Dominates the margin bog zone. Cuts skin easily; wear gloves. |
| **Peat Moss** | Bright green / rust-red ground cover | Grows over bog holes, concealing them. A bright-red carpet often means soft ground. |
| **Stone Pine** | Stunted, wind-sculpted conifers | Found only in sheltered valleys and below the ridge line. Never on the open moor. |
| **Marsh Alder** | Twisted multi-stemmed tree | Marks the bog margin. Where alder grows, the water table is within a foot of the surface. |
| **Moor Lichen** | Grey-white crusty patches on rock | Edible (barely). Emergency food. Dwarves make a rough gruel from it. |

### Hazardous Plants
| Plant | Hazard |
|---|---|
| **Corpse Bloom** | A pale yellow-white flower that grows over buried animal carcasses and Quag Mire overflow zones. Beautiful. Releases a spore cloud when touched that causes 1 hour of nausea (CON DC 12). |
| **Black Wort** | Dark green ground-hugging vine near bog margins. Contact causes mild rash (distracting but not dangerous). Poisonous if eaten. |
| **Strangler Sedge** | Found where bog cotton thins. Roots entangle ankles. DC 12 Athletics or be restrained until freed. |

### Valuable Plants (University Research Interest)
| Plant | Value |
|---|---|
| **Sepia Root** | A waxy underground tuber whose sap has mild coagulant properties. 5 gp/oz to an alchemist; 15 gp/oz to a physician. Found only in the high moor. |
| **Frost Yarrow** | Blooms in the first cold snap of autumn. Used in cold-resistance preparations. 10 gp/bundle. |
| **Bogwood** | Ancient semi-petrified wood pulled from deep bog. Extraordinarily dense; burns slowly; dwarf weaponsmiths prize it for handle-making. 2 gp/lb. |
| **Marsh Nightcap** | A small black fungus with a faint blue luminescence. Hallucinogenic. The Druid enclave near Ehlonna's Mount considers it sacred. Not for sale. |

The Sepia Uplands support a robust ecology structured around the moor, the valleys, and the bog margin.

### Mundane Wildlife

| **Upland Elk** | Valley edges | Large, calm in summer, highly aggressive in autumn rut. Bull elk are a genuine travel hazard in Harvester. |
| **Mountain Hare** | Moor and ridge | White in winter; mottled brown in summer. Primary prey for most raptors. |

| **Brown Bear** | Valley forest | Solitary. Enormous specimens in the eastern valleys. Avoid entirely. |
| **Osprey / Peregrine** | Ridges, lake shore | Common raptors. Frequently used by Rangers as navigation markers — if osprey are present, open water is within a mile. |

## VI. Peoples of the Sepia Uplands (576 CY)

### 🧔 The Perrenlanders (Human — Flan/Oeridian Mix)
The nearest civilization. Hearty, suspicious of outsiders, fiercely independent even from their own cantons. Those who live in the southern Sepia towns (Kottsmort, Horstham, Vostren) are miners and trappers, rougher than their lowland cousins. They speak Old Oeridian with a Flan accent, call the Quag Mires *"the Rot,"* and refer to Ritterstadt as *"that fool's palisade in the mud."* They are not wrong, but they respect courage and they trade fairly.

**Attitude toward the University:** Confused but cautiously favorable. An institution that pays cash for recovered relics and keeps the north roads safer is worth tolerating.

### ⛏️ The Clatspur Dwarves (Mountain Dwarf)
Two clanholds dominate the western uplands. **Brock's Aerie** sits atop the highest of the western Teeth, its gate visible for thirty miles on clear days. **Bitter Pass** guards the primary route between the uplands and the High Pass north of Perrenland. These are old, proud, self-sufficient clans who have mined the Clatspur ridges for at least 400 years. They trade ore and metalwork for preserved food, lumber (they have none), and information about nomad movements.

**Attitude toward the University:** Hogar Irongrip is a Bitter Pass dwarven exile — apprenticed out after a forge accident. The clans know he is in Ritterstadt. They have not come to collect him or send aid, which says something. Possibly several things.

### 🌲 The Upland Gnomes (Rock Gnome)
Concentrated in the eastern valley complex near mineral seams and the Vesve border. They are closer in culture to the Vesve's elves than to Perrenland and speak a dialect that mixes Gnomish with High Elven vowels. They are skilled engineers and prospectors and have carved extensive underground warrens beneath the eastern valleys. They are not hostile but are deeply private; uninvited visits to their settlements are strongly discouraged.

**Attitude toward the University:** Academic institutions are deeply interesting to them. A gnome scholar has, reportedly, been watching Ritterstadt's development from a distance.

### 🍃 The Halflings of the Southern Valleys
Lightfoot and Tallfellow halflings occupy the warmer wooded valleys of the southern Sepia. They are farmers, orchardists, beekeepers, and consummate traders. Their villages are deliberately hard to find. They have survived here by being overlooked, and they prefer to keep it that way. They know the valley paths better than any other group.

**Attitude toward the University:** Friendly and curious, with a commercial streak. They sell smoked provisions, honey-mead, and valley maps.

### 🌿 The Druid Enclave (Mixed Races)
A small but significant enclave of druids and their acolytes maintains a standing stone circle called **Ehlonna's Mount**, approximately 40 miles southeast of Ritterstadt. They are dedicated to Ehlonna and observe the old calendar of the Flan with great precision. They are the region's de facto authority on weather, seasonal cycles, and the health of the bog. Their opinion of the Blackfrost Bloom: grave concern.

**Attitude toward the University:** Neutral, watching. They do not oppose the settlement; they are not yet sure it belongs here.

### 🐴 The Wolf Nomads (Flan — Nomadic)
The eastern Sepia is within the migration corridor of the **Wolf Nomads**, who move south through the uplands in autumn and north again in late spring, following their horse herds and the game. They are not aggressive toward well-armed travelers who make no threatening moves, but they regard the entire uplands as their ancestral hunting ground and Ritterstadt as an encroachment. Individual outriders have been seen watching the palisade from the high moor. In 576 CY, the Nomad migration is active (noted on the Campaign Timepiece). Expect riders.

**Attitude toward the University:** Skeptical and potentially hostile. A formal meeting with a Nomad band leader is a high-stakes diplomatic encounter.

### 👁️ Agents of Iuz (Threat — Not a People)
The proximity of Iuz's territory to the northeast is a constant background concern. In 576 CY, Iuz is consolidating power and probing borders. His agents — humanoids, corrupted humans, the occasional cambion — occasionally cross through the eastern Sepia. They are not here in strength, but their presence means the eastern uplands are not entirely safe, and things found there may have a darker provenance than they appear.

## VII. Settlements & Notable Locations

### 🏡 Ritterstadt *(Campaign Hub)*
The newest settlement in the region. A palisaded hamlet at the northern edge of the Sepia Uplands where the margin bog begins. Founded 565 CY by The Four Founders to guard the Fiend's Embrace relic recovered from Cold Stone Keep. Population: growing from a skeleton crew toward fifty souls. The only settlement in the region with an adventuring college.

### 🏘️ Traft *(Canon — Southern Approach)*
The last town before the Clatspur passes. A Perrenlander market town of perhaps 400 souls. Caravan staging point; has an inn, a smith, a temple to Zilchus, and a garrison of ten canton guards who do not patrol north of the town walls. Four days' travel south of Ritterstadt by established track.

### ⛏️ Horstham *(Canon — Central-Western Sepia)*
The largest human settlement in the uplands proper — a rough mining town of perhaps 200 permanent residents that swells to 400 in summer. Built around a productive iron seam in the western ridges. Has a smithy, two taverns, a factor's house (Perrenland merchant interests), and a population of very tough people who find most outside threats underwhelming by local standards.

### 🏔️ Kottsmort *(Canon — Northern Sepia)*
A small trapper's outpost of 60–80 residents occupying a fortified longhouse complex near the edge of the margin bog. Kottsmort is the closest settlement of any size to Ritterstadt — approximately 25 miles east across the high moor. It is also the source of most current information about the Blackfrost Bloom's effects, as its residents have spent the last winter inside watching the marsh creep closer.

### 🌿 Vostren *(Canon — Eastern Valleys)*
A valley settlement in the eastern Sepia, near the gnome territories. Mixed human-gnome population, perhaps 150 souls. Known for its mining output (copper and tin) and for a weekly market that attracts traders from as far as the Vesve border. The gnomes trust it enough to trade there directly, which is unusual.

### 🏰 Brock's Aerie *(Canon — Western Teeth)*
The higher of the two dwarf strongholds. Carved into a crest of the Teeth at approximately 3,200 feet. Essentially impregnable. Clan Brock has held this position for eleven generations. They watch the western approaches to the Uplands and theoretically warn Perrenland of threats from the north — though their warning schedule is on their own terms.

### 🗡️ Bitter Pass *(Canon — Northern Teeth)*
The northern dwarf fortress, guarding the pass between the high moor and the Lake Quag lowlands. Smaller than Brock's Aerie but more strategically placed. Clan Irongrip — Hogar's people — holds this position. The fact that a Clan Irongrip youth is sitting in Ritterstadt as an unsanctioned apprentice-turned-master will eventually require addressing.

## VIII. The Quag Mires — The Northern Frontier

> [!note] Not to be confused with the **Cold Marshes**
> The canonical Cold Marshes of the Flanaess lie roughly **250 miles northeast** of Ritterstadt, beyond the Wolf Nomads' steppes toward the Burneal Forest and Blackmoor. The marsh country described below is the **Quag Mires** — the local fenland north of Lake Quag. Perrenlanders call it *"the Rot."* Whether the Quag Mires' entropic seep and the distant Cold Marshes share a common cause is a question the druids of Ehlonna's Mount decline to answer in public.

What lies north of the margin bog is not the Sepia Uplands anymore. The **Quag Mires** are a distinct and far more dangerous region: a vast expanse of fens, bogs, and shallow acidic lakes stretching from the Sepia's edge north toward the Rovers' Barrens and the territory of Iuz. They are the source of the Dulsi and Opicm Rivers and the origin of most of the supernatural threats that press against Ritterstadt's palisade.

The Quag Mires are *never* safe. Not even in summer. Key characteristics:

- **Temperature:** Even in Flocktime, the air in the deep marsh runs 10–15°F colder than the Sepia plateau due to water evaporation and magical cold residue from the Blackfrost Bloom.
- **Ground:** Unstable at all times. No wheeled travel. Foot travel requires poles and guides.
- **Visibility:** Fog is near-permanent in the lower channels. Even on clear days, the evaporation haze limits sight to 200–300 feet over open water.
- **Water:** All surface water in the Quag Mires is slightly acidic and carries a faint black tint from decomposed organic matter. Drinking it raw causes 1d4 CON damage (no save) and a day of sickness. Boiling helps but does not fully neutralize it.
- **Undead:** The Quag Mires produce undead organically — bodies interred in the peat animate over time, accelerated by proximity to Iuz's influence. Do not camp at ground level without setting a perimeter.
- **Navigation:** The marsh channels shift seasonally. A route that worked in Flocktime may be 6 feet underwater in Coldeven. Accurate maps of the Quag Mires go stale in one season.

**Key phrase:** *"Grey reeds without end, cold haze over black water — and no birdsong at all."*

> [!danger] **Cold Stone Keep**
> Somewhere in the Quag Mires north of Ritterstadt lies the ruins of **Cold Stone Keep** — the site where the Four Founders recovered the Fiend's Embrace in 565 CY. Its exact location is not reliably mapped. The sounds heard from its direction on still nights are considered by Ritterstadt's residents to be a wind phenomenon. This is almost certainly incorrect.

## IX. The Blackfrost Bloom *(Active Threat — 576 CY)*

> [!danger] **Active Global Effect — Blackfrost Bloom**
> *Current status as of 12th Planting, 576 CY: Active. Suppressing temperatures 3–4 weeks behind seasonal norm. Margin bog stability degraded. Quag Mire boundary advancing.*

### What It Is
The Blackfrost Bloom is a cyclical magical phenomenon that originates somewhere deep within the Quag Mires — possibly connected to the residual energy of the **Rain of Colorless Fire** and the **Invoked Devastation** that poisoned the northwestern Flanaess over two centuries ago. Roughly every 11–15 years, conditions in the marsh align to produce what the druids of Ehlonna's Mount call *the Bloom*: a period of weeks to months during which a cold, colorless magical frost spreads outward from the deepest marsh channels, blighting new vegetation, killing livestock, deepening bogs, and activating otherwise-dormant undead.

### Observable Signs
- **Black Ice in Summer:** Thin sheets of black-tinted ice forming on standing water during what should be frost-free months. Unlike normal ice, it does not melt in sunlight until noon.
- **Bloom Crystals:** Small translucent crystals forming on stone and organic matter near affected areas. They are cold to the touch, faintly luminescent at night, and crumble if warmed. University researchers believe they have alchemical value (research ongoing — no confirmed classification yet).
- **Vegetation Die-Back:** The pale corpse bloom flower proliferates during a Blackfrost year. Healthy heather moors develop grey patches where the Bloom's cold kills the roots.
- **Behavioral Changes in Animals:** Predators move south earlier than usual. Bog otters abandon territory. Wolves run in larger packs than normal, suggesting prey is scarce in the deeper marsh.
- **Undead Pressure:** Bog zombies and marsh-drowned are encountered closer to the palisade than in non-Bloom years.

### What Nobody Knows (Yet)
The Bloom's origin has never been definitively identified. The druids believe it is a wound in the world — the earth itself bleeding cold through an old scar left by the Rain of Colorless Fire. The Ritterstadt founders believed it was connected to the Fiend's Embrace. Neither theory has been confirmed. The fact that 576 CY sees a Bloom coinciding with Ritterstadt's first active expedition season may or may not be coincidental.

## X. Exploration Sites — The Sepia Uplands & Margin

The following sites have been identified as potential expedition targets from Ritterstadt. None has been fully explored. Classification follows the University Mission Type system.

---

### 🟡 Site 01 — The Rust Seams *(Margin Bog, ~6 miles NNW)*
**Mission Type:** ⚒️ Civic Project / 📜 Field Thesis
**Danger Level:** 1
**What's Known:** A zone of iron-rich groundwater surfacing in the bog margin, staining the peat rust-red. Hogar Irongrip has identified it as potentially extractable bog iron — raw but workable. The University's strategic interest is obvious: Ritterstadt's smithy cannot function without raw material.
**What's Suspected:** The red color is unusually vivid. Bog iron is typically dull rust; this is bright crimson. Unknown whether this is a natural mineral variant or something else.
**The Complication:** The zone is inside bog troll territory. The troll — possibly two — hasn't attacked Ritterstadt, but has destroyed two previous attempts at surface collection.

---

### 🟠 Site 02 — The Standing Stones of the High Moor *(~18 miles E)*
**Mission Type:** 📜 Field Thesis
**Danger Level:** 2
**What's Known:** A circle of eleven standing stones on the open moor, visible from the eastern ridge. Pre-Flan in construction — possibly ancient Flan or older. The stones are carved with weathered symbols that do not match any known Flan, Suel, or Oeridian script. One stone has fallen and cracked; the Druid enclave near Ehlonna's Mount reportedly will not approach closer than a mile.
**The Complication:** The Wolf Nomad migration route passes directly through this site. The nomads treat it as sacred ground and do not permit strangers near it during migration season. Outside migration season, the site is unguarded — but the nomads will know if it has been disturbed.

---

### 🔴 Site 03 — The Frozen Library of Bitter Shelf *(~28 miles NW, ridge approach)*
**Mission Type:** 📜 Field Thesis
**Danger Level:** 3
**What's Known:** A natural shelf of rock on the high face of the Teeth contains what appears to be an ancient cache of Suloise stonework — carved tablets, sealed containers, and the remains of a small stone structure. Visible from the ridge path but inaccessible without climbing gear. The Suloise were present in the northern Flanaess before the Rain of Colorless Fire; what they left behind in a place this remote is an open question.
**The Complication:** The ledge is in the hunting territory of the harpy pair that occupies the ridge. The climb itself is DC 16 Athletics (Clatspur rock, wind-polished). And Clan Brock of Brock's Aerie knows the shelf exists and has, thus far, chosen not to excavate it. Their reasons are unknown.

### 🔴 Site 04 — The Drowned Village of Kessmark *(~15 miles N, margin bog)*
**Mission Type:** ⚔️ Frontier Vigil / 📜 Field Thesis
**Danger Level:** 3
**What's Known:** Kottsmort trappers report the rooftops of an old village visible above the waterline in a broad bog channel north of Ritterstadt. The village — called *Kessmark* in local memory — was lost to the Quag Mires in approximately 512 CY, during the last major advance of the bog. Perrenlander records suggest it was a farming settlement of forty to sixty families. That means forty to sixty families' worth of possessions, valuables, and possibly records drowned in place.
**The Complication:** It also means forty to sixty families' worth of undead, preserved by the bog and activated by the Blackfrost Bloom. The rooftops are visible because the Bloom's cold has partially re-frozen the channel — which has also driven the Kessmark dead upward through the ice.

### 🟣 Site 05 — Ehlonna's Mount and the Druid Conclave *(~40 miles SE)*
**Mission Type:** 📜 Field Thesis (Diplomatic)
**Danger Level:** 1 (travel) / Special (political)
**What's Known:** The standing stone circle maintained by the Druid enclave is a significant site for understanding the Sepia's history and the Blackfrost Bloom. The druids possess centuries of observational records. They are not hostile.
**The Complication:** They are also not welcoming. The correct approach is uncertain. Arriving with force, arriving with obvious academic interest in extraction, or arriving during a ceremony would each go badly in different ways. What they want from Ritterstadt — if anything — has not been established.

---

### ⚫ Site 06 — Cold Stone Keep *(Quag Mires, location unconfirmed)*
**Mission Type:** Special — requires University sanctioning above standard level
**Danger Level:** Unknown / High
**What's Known:** Ritterstadt was built to guard what was *taken* from this place. The Founders mapped it but the maps were never reproduced; the originals are locked in the Founder archive. Someone in Ritterstadt almost certainly knows where it is.
**What's Not Known:** Whether it has been reoccupied. What else was left there. Why the Founders sealed the map.
