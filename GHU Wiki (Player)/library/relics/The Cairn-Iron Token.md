# The Cairn-Iron Token

> [!info] Quick Reference
> **Type:** Wondrous item (token) | **Rarity:** Uncommon | **Attunement:** No
> **Origin:** F2 - The Weeping Cairns — Flan grave-goods
> **Status:** ⚫ Held by the dead; given freely, or stolen at peril

A flat disc of dark iron the size of a large coin, stamped with a Flan clan-mark — a sigil of lineage and authority, buried with a chieftain so that their standing followed them into the earth. The iron is cold, heavy beyond its size, and faintly damp with the grief-water that weeps from the cairns. It is, to the Flan, not treasure but *office* — and how you come by it changes entirely what it is.

---

## Properties — If Freely Given

A token *gifted* by the grief of the Weeping Cairns (see the site's "courtesy" — sit with the stones, make an offering, show genuine respect) carries the dead's blessing:

- **Standing Among the Dead.** You have advantage on Charisma checks made to parley with, turn, or command Flan undead and ancestor-spirits, including the Cairn-Hound and the spirits of the Ancestor Barrow. The Flan dead recognize the mark as one carried with honour.
- **Grief-Ward.** Once per long rest, as a reaction to taking necrotic or cold damage from an undead, reduce that damage by 2d8 as the cold grief turns aside from one it acknowledges.
- **A Voice with the Beckenhaus.** The token is recognized by Flan-descended families (the Beckenhaus among them) as proof you keep the old courtesies — a meaningful social key on the frontier.

---

## The Curse — If Stolen

> [!danger] Taken, Not Given
> A token *robbed* from a cairn is a different object entirely. The thief is marked: the Cairn-Hound of that field gains Grief-Scent on them permanently, and even far from the moor the thief suffers — disadvantage on the first save each day, and a recurring night-scratching at door and window (a manifestation of the slighted dead) that denies the benefit of a long rest on a failed DC 12 Wisdom save. The curse lifts **only** when the token is returned to its cairn with apology. Many a frontier ghost-story begins with someone who "just took a little iron coin."
