# The Calendar & Festivals

The Flanaess uses a **364-day calendar** divided into **twelve months of 28 days each** and **four festival weeks of 7 days each**, placed at the turning points of the seasons. The year has a rhythm that matters: on the northern frontier, the difference between Harvester and Ready'reat is the difference between comfortable and desperate.

The current date is **12th of Planting, 576 CY**. The Blackfrost Bloom has pushed temperatures three to four weeks behind seasonal norms. By the calendar, Planting should mean muddy fields and apple blossoms. At present, it means frost at night and killing winds off Lake Quag.

| **Coldeven** | Early Spring | 28 | The thaw begins — unevenly, with treachery underfoot. |
| **Planting** | Late Spring | 28 | Fields go in. New arrivals appear. The road opens again. ← *You are here.* |
| **Flocktime** | Early Summer | 28 | Livestock moves to upland pasture. University expeditions peak. |
| **Wealsun** | High Summer | 28 | The longest days. The moor is passable but the bogs are unpredictable. |
| **Reaping** | Late Summer | 28 | Harvest begins. Every hand is needed. Expeditions wind down. |
| **Goodmonth** | Early Autumn | 28 | The best weather of the year. Brief and not to be wasted. |
| **Harvester** | Harvest | 28 | The autumn slaughter. Smoke from every chimney. Winter prep begins. |
| **Patchwall** | Late Autumn | 28 | Named for patching — roofs, walls, boots, arguments. |
| **Ready'reat** | Early Winter | 28 | Named for readiness. The question is always: *are you?* |
| **Sunsebb** | Deep Winter | 28 | The sun sets before the fourth bell. The hamlet goes quiet. |

## The Four Festival Weeks

Between seasons, **Needfest**, **Growfest**, **Richfest**, and **Brewfest** each run for seven days. These are not idle holidays — in the Sepia Uplands, a festival week is communal labor with singing.

### Needfest — *Midwinter (after Sunsebb)*
The darkest festival. Seven days of indoor celebration, storytelling, and ward-against-the-cold ritual. On the frontier, Needfest is when you count your stores, your neighbors, and your reasons to stay. Pelor's light is invoked nightly. Fires are kept burning from dusk to dawn.

**In Ritterstadt:** The Big Barn serves as the festival hall. Everyone eats from the communal pot. Gifts are practical: preserved food, mended gear, candles. Vater Sigmund conducts a long-night vigil on the last night of Needfest — the Watch Against the Dark — which draws the entire hamlet.

### Growfest — *Early Spring (after Coldeven)*
The planting festival. Seven days of preparation rituals, seed blessings, and prayer to **Beory** (the Oerth Mother) and **Ehlonna** (Lady of the Forests) for a growing season that cooperates. Druids of the Old Faith visit the standing stone sites on the high moor. Farmers leave small offerings at field margins.

**In Ritterstadt:** The east forest shrine is the focal point. Even those who follow Pholtus or St. Cuthbert tend to participate in the Growfest field-blessing, because pragmatism outweighs theology when the crop is at stake.

### Richfest — *Midsummer (after Wealsun)*
Seven days dedicated to **Pelor**, the Sun Father. The longest days of the year. Healers are busy (people overdo it). Marriages are announced. The University traditionally schedules its most ambitious expeditions to begin in the week immediately following Richfest, when daylight is still generous.

**In Ritterstadt:** The festival is quieter than in southern towns — the Sepia Uplands doesn't lend itself to prolonged outdoor celebration. A bonfire on the first night, a communal meal at midweek, and prayers at dawn on the last day.

### Brewfest — *Harvest (after Harvester)*
The loudest festival. Seven days marking the end of harvest, testing the year's ale, and celebrating survival. The autumn slaughter at The Big Barn typically falls in the last days of Harvester and bleeds into Brewfest proper. The job board at the Fiend's Rest goes quiet — everyone is busy.

**In Ritterstadt:** Brewfest is the social event of the year. Lotte Kesselring opens the Grey Wolf's back storeroom, which holds casks that are only broached once annually. Old grievances are aired and sometimes resolved. New arrivals who participate fully in Brewfest are considered to have earned their first real footing in the community.

## Days of the Week

Each of the 28-day months is divided into **four seven-day weeks**. The days, in order:

**Starday — Sunday — Moonday — Godsday — Waterday — Earthday — Freeday**

**Godsday** is the day of prayer and rest, observed with varying strictness. **Freeday** is the traditional market day where it exists. On the frontier, both days look almost identical to the others — there is always work.

---

## Time in Practice

Common folk of the Sepia Uplands track time by season, month, and rough week rather than by precise date. If you ask a farmer what day it is, he will tell you *"mid-Planting, third week"* — not the 19th. Exact dates are a scholar's concern. The University maintains a precise calendar in the Academic Registry, and Vater Sigmund keeps a dated journal. Everyone else goes by the light.
