## 🌟 Overview

Gruumsh is the creator and patron deity of the orcish people — the one-eyed god of conquest, survival, and the fundamental claim that strength has the right to take what it can hold. He is one of the most actively hostile deities in the Flanaess in terms of his direct encouragement of mortal violence, and he is worshipped by all orcs to some degree whether they personally embrace his values or not — the cultural weight of Gruumsh's tradition makes rejection of it a form of racial apostasy that most orcish communities will not tolerate.

The eye in his symbol is the eye he still has — the one Corellon did not take. In orcish mythology, Gruumsh was blinded in one eye by Corellon Larethian in their primordial conflict, and this wound is the defining fact of his divine existence. He did not stop fighting when he was blinded; he did not stop existing; he did not forgive; he does not sleep, because he does not trust the universe enough to become vulnerable. The hatred between orcish and elven peoples is rooted directly in the divine conflict between Gruumsh and Corellon, and it is sustained at the divine level in a way that makes it resistant to mortal reconciliation.

He appears as an enormous orc in black armor, one eye covered with a patch or simply gone, the remaining eye burning red with the specific light of something that has been angry for so long that anger has become indistinguishable from the baseline of existence. He holds a spear. He is always ready to fight.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

1. **The strong take; the weak give.** Gruumsh's theology is built on this principle in its simplest form. He does not try to make it more comfortable. The world belongs to those who can hold it, and the orcish people are strong enough to take what they need. Other races having things is a problem to be solved.
2. **Never show weakness.** The wound that Gruumsh carries is not concealed — it is displayed as evidence of survival rather than defeat. He continues. This is the core orcish spiritual teaching under Gruumsh: what matters is not whether you are hurt but whether you continue. Continuing is the only requirement.
3. **War is worship.** Military success is the primary devotional act in Gruumsh's tradition. Victory in battle is prayer answered; defeat is the indication that the defeated party did not pray hard enough, or fight hard enough, or deserve Gruumsh's favor. The theology is brutal and produces the behavior it teaches.

## ⛪ The Clergy

Gruumsh's clergy are warlord-priests — they lead in battle, conduct the war-rituals before significant campaigns, and serve as the spiritual authorities of orcish communities. They are expected to be the most capable fighters in the community; a Gruumsh priest who is not respected as a warrior is a contradiction in terms that orcish communities resolve through either rejection or violence.

The specific role of Gruumsh's clergy within orcish political structure varies enormously by community: in some, the warlord and the priest are the same person; in others, they are separate offices in tension with each other; in some communities, the priestly tradition has developed significant independence from the warlord structure and serves as a check on it.

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The One-Eyes
Gruumsh's most devoted mortal champions — warriors who sacrifice an eye to their god as a demonstration of commitment, literally mirroring Gruumsh's wound. The One-Eyes are considered especially blessed by Gruumsh (or especially damaged, depending on who is doing the assessment) and serve as shock troops and champions in significant orcish military operations. The spiritual transformation that accompanies the sacrifice is understood as a direct connection to Gruumsh's divine experience — to lose an eye and continue fighting is to do what the god did.

### The Shattered Spear
A tradition within the faith for Gruumsh priests who specialize in large-scale military campaigns — the theological backing, logistical support, and morale maintenance for significant orcish war-making. The Shattered Spear's involvement in an orcish military operation indicates that the campaign has divine endorsement and the full backing of the faith's resources.

---

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

**The Night of Teeth (new moon):** The faith's primary operational holy day — a night designated for raiding and military action. The darkest night of the month is when Gruumsh's favor is most easily invoked, and orcish communities with strong Gruumsh tradition use it as the primary night for raids on neighboring settlements. The Night of Teeth is dreaded by communities that live near significant orcish populations.

**The Feast of the Eye:** The anniversary of Gruumsh's blinding is observed as a day of institutional rage — ceremonies that involve the explicit rehearsal of the elf-orc conflict, the honoring of Gruumsh's endurance in the face of his wound, and often increased military activity against elves or elven-allied communities. The Feast is both genuine religious observance and useful motivation for military action.

---

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### Nishrek (Acheron)
Gruumsh's divine realm — a plane of constant battle between orcish souls, a war-heaven that the orcish dead go to fight in eternally. Nishrek is not comfortable by human or elven standards, but it is the faith's model of paradise: perpetual combat, perpetual survival, perpetual proving of worth through force.

### The Throne of Bloody Conquest (Great Kingdom ruins / Pomarj)
Major orcish temples in the material plane tend to be found in territories controlled by significant orcish populations. The Pomarj — a peninsula now largely controlled by orcish and goblinoid populations — has the most significant temple complexes in the current Flanaess.

## 🎲 Player's Guide

Gruumsh's faith is primarily encountered as an antagonist institution. Understanding it helps adventurers understand orcish military behavior, predict Night of Teeth timing, assess the significance of One-Eye warriors (target priority or extreme caution, depending on circumstances), and engage diplomatically with orcish communities where the military situation permits. A half-orc character from a Gruumsh-tradition community carries significant cultural baggage regardless of their personal alignment — the faith's claim on orcish identity is comprehensive and rejection of it has social consequences. A nuanced half-orc character may be working through the relationship between the tradition they come from and the values they have chosen.
