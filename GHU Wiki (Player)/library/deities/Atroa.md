## 🌟 Overview

Atroa is the Oeridian goddess of spring — the season's end of winter, the east wind bringing warmth, and the renewal that follows a difficult passage. She is one of four Oeridian wind-deities (with Velnius overseeing sky and weather generally, Telchur commanding the cold north wind of winter, and Wenta governing the west wind of autumn), and she is the one worshippers are most glad to see return. She is the daughter of Procan, god of the seas — the east wind comes from the sea, carrying moisture and the promise of growth.

She appears as a young woman in white and green, perpetually in the first warmth of morning, with flowers opening in her wake. Her expression is cheerful without being frivolous — spring is not trivial; it is necessary. The songbird on her heart-symbol represents new life calling out to announce itself.

Her faith is seasonal and agricultural in its primary expression. The period from late winter to mid-spring is Atroa's domain in the ritual calendar of Oeridian communities, and she is one of the deities most actively invoked by people who are not her dedicated worshippers — because everyone who plants a field or begins a new endeavor has reason to ask for her attention.

---

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

1. **Renewal is not automatic — it is earned.** Spring does not simply happen; it happens because the world has worked through winter. The faith teaches that new beginnings require the completion of what came before, not the abandonment of it. One does not start fresh by pretending the past did not happen; one starts fresh by processing it.
2. **Love is the east wind.** Atroa's portfolio includes love in its seasonal character — the love that arrives with warmth, that opens things that have been closed. It is not the deep sustaining love of long partnership (that is more Myhriss's territory); it is the love that makes things possible, that creates the conditions for growth.
3. **Give the new season what it needs.** The faith's practical ethic is a farming ethic: you do not choose whether spring comes, but you do choose whether you are ready for it. Preparation, readiness to receive, and willingness to plant when the conditions are right are the practical expressions of Atroa's doctrine.

## ⛪ The Clergy

Atroa's clergy are primarily found in agricultural communities where the turn of the seasons has direct material consequence. They serve as community ritual-leaders during planting season, provide blessings for new endeavors, conduct the healing and recovery work associated with winter's end (spring is when the sick who survived winter often finally recover, or finally don't), and maintain the faith's modest temple network.

The clergy wear white and green, with flowers incorporated into vestments during the holy days. They are among the more cheerful clergy in the pantheon, which reflects the faith's portfolio — but the experienced ones have also sat with people who did not make it through winter, and carry the weight of that alongside the lightness.

There is no formal higher hierarchy beyond local senior priests. Atroa's faith does not produce religious institutions in the way that more law-focused faiths do; it produces community relationships.

---

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

**The First Thaw:** When the east wind first carries genuine warmth — typically late winter before the astronomical spring — Atroa's faithful gather to formally acknowledge winter's end. This is a quiet observance, often a single morning gathering, more relief than celebration. Healers make their rounds; the community takes stock of who is here and who is not.

**Blossom Day (mid-spring):** The major festival — a full community celebration of planting and renewal. Seeds are blessed, new partnerships are announced (business and personal), recovered patients are celebrated publicly, and the community's new endeavors for the year are formally committed to in a communal ceremony. Atroa's clergy preside; the celebration typically extends well into the evening.

## 🎲 Player's Guide

Atroa's church is most accessible in agricultural communities during planting season. Outside of that context, her temples can provide healing (the Life domain produces capable healers), particularly for recovery from long illness or difficult passages — the faith specializes in the kind of healing that follows sustained difficulty rather than acute injury. A cleric of Atroa is a healer-support character with a strong thematic connection to recovery, new beginnings, and the natural cycle. The Nature domain adds wilderness capability appropriate for a deity whose portfolio is explicitly seasonal.
