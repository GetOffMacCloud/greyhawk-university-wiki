## 🌟 Overview

Ehlonna is the Flanaess's primary deity of the good-aligned woodland — the goddess who governs the forest when it is a place of life, sustenance, and beauty rather than danger and darkness. She occupies a middle position in the nature pantheon: more accessible than Obad-Hai's strict neutrality, more directly benevolent than Beory's remote serenity, and more specifically focused on the human relationship with the forest than the elven woodland deities.

Her symbol is the unicorn horn — a choice that communicates her theology precisely. The unicorn is a woodland creature of extraordinary beauty and magic; it is powerful, shy, and protective; it approaches only those whose hearts are genuinely good. Ehlonna does not teach that the forest is for everyone indiscriminately. It is for those who approach it correctly, with respect and genuine love, and who understand that the relationship between a person and the forest is one of stewardship, not ownership.

She appears in sacred imagery as a beautiful woman in green and gold, walking barefoot through forest growth, accompanied by woodland animals — deer, foxes, birds — that approach her willingly. The imagery emphasizes accessibility and warmth: Ehlonna is a deity of the forest, but she is also a deity of the people who live alongside the forest, and her faith is welcoming in a way that some nature religions are not.

Her faith has significant overlap with elven religious traditions — Arvandor, the elven heaven, is where Ehlonna makes her home, and she is revered by many elves and half-elves who find her more personally accessible than Corellon Larethian's more formal tradition. In communities where elves and humans live in proximity, Ehlonna's shrine is often the shared sacred space.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

### The Fundamental Tenets

Ehlonna's faith is organized around three principles called the Three Green Truths:

1. **The forest sustains all who respect it.** Every hunter, every forester, every farmer at the forest's edge depends on the forest's continued health. The faith teaches that taking from the forest in sustainable ways is not merely permitted but celebrated — the hunter who takes only what is needed, the woodcutter who replants what is cut, the farmer who leaves the forest's edge intact, are all practitioners of Ehlonna's faith whether they know it or not.
2. **All woodland creatures are sacred.** Animals are not merely resources. They are participants in the forest's life. Cruelty to animals, hunting for sport without purpose, and the destruction of ecosystems for convenience are violations of the faith's most fundamental commitments.
3. **The forest's edge is a place of meeting, not conflict.** Between the civilized world and the wild, there is a boundary that Ehlonna's faith exists to tend. It should be porous — the forest accessible to those who respect it, the village protected by the forest's presence, and the relationship between them one of mutual benefit rather than mutual fear.

### What the Faith Preaches to Ordinary Worshippers

The church's message to farmers, villagers, and small-town communities is practical and immediate: the forest is your neighbor, not your enemy. Respect its boundaries, take only what you need, and the forest will provide — game, timber, medicinal plants, clean water, protection from the elements. Disrespect it, and you will pay the price. The faith provides a theological framework for what many communities already practice intuitively.

For hunters specifically, the faith provides ritual structure for the hunt — prayers before, gratitude after, the understanding that the animal's life is a gift that carries obligations. Hunter communities who follow Ehlonna tend to practice sustainable, respectful hunting as a matter of religious commitment as well as practical necessity.

### What the Faith Demands of the Devout

From clergy, rangers, and dedicated worshippers, the faith demands: active stewardship of specific forest areas, intervention against those who damage the forest carelessly or maliciously, care for wounded or threatened woodland animals, and the provision of healing and guidance to travelers who need it in the wilderness.

The faith is explicitly not demanding about formal observance — Ehlonna does not require temples and liturgy from those who live in the forest. What she requires is relationship: consistent, attentive, respectful interaction with the natural world.

## ⛪ The Clergy

### Becoming a Priest

Ehlonna's clergy come from two traditions: druids who align with the faith's goodness (rather than the strict neutrality of Obad-Hai's druidic tradition) and clerics with a nature orientation. The faith does not require urban training — many of Ehlonna's most effective clergy have never spent significant time in cities and have learned through apprenticeship in the forest with an established ranger or cleric of the faith.

### Ranks & Hierarchy

- **Grove-Keeper:** Entry-level clergy. Responsible for a specific grove, shrine, or forest area.
- **Woodland Shepherd:** Full working clergy. Serves a community adjacent to significant forest.
- **Forest Warden:** Senior clergy. Commands significant ecclesiastical authority and typically has extensive wilderness experience.
- **High Warden:** Regional authority. Oversees the church's activities in a major forest region.
- **The Grand Shepherd:** The church's supreme leadership — typically a council of High Wardens rather than a single leader, reflecting the faith's distributed, non-hierarchical orientation.

### Duties & Obligations

Active stewardship, provision of healing to injured animals and travelers, mediation of conflicts between communities and the forest (both when communities are damaging the forest and when the forest — through dangerous animals or plants — is threatening communities), and the training of hunters and foresters in respectful practice.

### Vestments & Appearance

Green and gold, reflecting the forest in spring and autumn. The vestments are practical rather than elaborate — designed for forest travel, not temple ceremony. The unicorn horn symbol appears on cloaks, carved into wooden pendants, or worn as bone-carved jewelry. Clergy rarely wear metal armor by preference; leather is the standard.

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The Foresters
The church's primary working order — rangers and clerics who serve as the practical guardians of specific forested areas. Foresters patrol, provide aid to travelers, intervene against illegal or destructive activity in the forest, and serve as the primary contact between forest communities and civilization.

### The Unicorn Circle
A more mystical order within the faith — practitioners who have developed a genuine relationship with unicorns and through them with the purest expressions of Ehlonna's portfolio. Unicorn Circle members are considered to have achieved a level of personal goodness that unicorns find trustworthy, which is the faith's most significant endorsement of character. They serve primarily as spiritual counselors and as mediators in conflicts where questions of genuine moral goodness are central.

### The Meadow-Walkers
An itinerant tradition within the faith — clergy who travel between communities rather than maintaining fixed positions, providing seasonal religious services, delivering news between forest communities, and keeping the faith's dispersed membership connected.

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

**The Green Festival (spring):** The major holy day — ceremonies in sacred groves across the Flanaess, celebrating the forest's renewal. New Grove-Keepers are formally invested, new shrines are consecrated, and the community relationship with the forest is formally renewed. The Green Festival is the one time of year when the faith's dispersed members gather in significant numbers.

**The Hunter's Moon (autumn):** A night of thanks for the hunt's success through the season. Hunters bring the first game of the autumn season to the shrine as an offering; the clergy bless the coming winter hunting and ask Ehlonna's guidance in taking only what is needed. It is one of the most widely observed holy days in frontier and rural communities, regardless of formal church membership.

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### The Gnarley Forest Groves
Ehlonna's primary sacred area in the central Flanaess — a series of ancient groves deep in the Gnarley Forest that have been maintained as sacred sites since the Flan period. Each grove is tended by a Grove-Keeper and serves as a regional sacred center. The largest, in the Gnarley's heart, is where the Grand Shepherd's council occasionally meets.

### The Celene Border Shrines
A network of shrines along the border of Celene — the elven kingdom — that serve the Ehlonna worshippers among both the human borderlands communities and the elves themselves. The shrines are jointly maintained by the church and by the elven faithful, and represent the faith's most significant formal connection to elven religious tradition.

## ⚖️ Divine Relations

**Allies:** Obad-Hai (complex — they share the nature portfolio but approach it differently; cooperation is more common than conflict), Corellon Larethian (her home in Arvandor reflects a genuine relationship with the elven divine tradition), Pelor (good and nature align naturally).

**Rivals:** Beory (the World Mother is more fundamental; Ehlonna is more specific; they don't conflict but don't actively cooperate).

**Enemies:** Iuz (who destroys forests in conquered territory), Erythnul (slaughter of animals and ecosystems), any power that treats nature as a resource to be consumed without regard for renewal.

## 🎲 Player's Guide to This Faith

### Seeking Help From This Church

Ehlonna's church is generous to those who approach it with genuine respect and clear need. It provides healing, wilderness guidance, access to forest resources, and mediation with the forest's inhabitants (including, occasionally, fey and forest spirits). It asks that you approach with respect for the forest and honest intention.

### What the Church Wants

- **Protection of specific forest areas** from logging, mining, or other destructive activities.
- **Rescue of endangered woodland creatures** from hunters, trappers, or magical threats.
- **Mediation** between communities damaging the forest (usually inadvertently) and the forest's defenders.
- **Investigation of desecrated groves** and the destruction of whatever caused the desecration.

### Clerical Advice

The Ehlonna cleric or ranger is the party's wilderness specialist with divine backing. Nature and Life domains make for an excellent healer-naturalist combination. The unicorn horn symbol is one of the most immediately legible signals of good character in the Flanaess's symbolic vocabulary; NPCs who recognize it will respond positively in most contexts outside Iuz's territory.
