## 🌟 Overview

Wee Jas is the Suel goddess of magic and death — and the combination of those two portfolios is the key to understanding her. She is not Nerull's death: she is not murder, darkness, or the hatred of life. She is death as a legal institution, death as the proper and orderly end of every life's contract, death as a process that must be governed correctly if the cosmos is to function. She is the Ruby Sorceress: beautiful, cold, precise, vain in the specific way of someone who has earned the right to vanity, and utterly committed to the proper ordering of things.

Her church is strongly associated with the Suel people — the ancient, pale-skinned magical civilization that predates most of the current Flanaess's cultures and whose remnants carry both extraordinary magical traditions and significant cultural arrogance. The church of Wee Jas tends to reflect both. Wee Jas's clerics are among the most technically accomplished magical practitioners in the church system of any faith, and they are aware of this.

Her symbol — a skull with a red gem replacing one eye — captures the paradox of her portfolio: the skull is death; the ruby is beauty, value, and the fire of magical talent. Together they represent her core teaching: that death and magic are not opposed but intertwined, that the proper study of death is a form of magical scholarship, and that both should be approached with rigor, precision, and appropriate respect for form.

Wee Jas's church occupies an unusual position in the alignment landscape. She is Lawful Neutral — not good, not evil — and her theology reflects this genuinely. Her clerics are not villains; they are not heroes; they are people for whom the law of death and the law of magic are the two most important organizing principles of existence, and who apply both with equal commitment and equal coldness. Communities that need a deity of death who isn't actively evil often find themselves with Wee Jas temples, because she is the closest thing the pantheon offers.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

### The Fundamental Tenets

Wee Jas's church organizes its doctrine around four principles it calls the Codes of the Sorceress:

1. **Death is the law.** Every living thing has a term, and that term ends. The church's role is not to oppose this but to manage it correctly — to ensure that death comes when it should (not early through murder, not late through unnatural extension), that the dead are treated with appropriate dignity, and that the transition from life to whatever follows is conducted in accordance with proper form.
2. **Magic is the discipline of mind over matter.** Magical talent is a gift and an obligation — those who have it are responsible for developing it rigorously and using it in accordance with law. The misuse of magical talent (careless casting, unauthorized necromancy, destructive use of magic without proper governance) is a violation of the gift and is treated seriously.
3. **Vanity, properly understood, is virtue.** Wee Jas's church has an unusual attitude toward pride in one's appearance and achievements. The faith teaches that appropriate pride — taking proper care of oneself, acknowledging genuine excellence, maintaining appropriate dignity — is healthy and correct. It is only vanity that exceeds one's actual achievement, or that substitutes for genuine substance, that is a failing.
4. **Law governs the boundary between life and death.** The undead are a violation of the law of death — not evil in themselves, but they represent a failure of the proper process. The church's position on undead is complex: it practices necromancy, it works with the dead, but it regards uncontrolled undead (those not properly created, governed, and eventually laid to rest through correct procedure) as a legal problem rather than a theological one.

### What the Faith Preaches to Ordinary Worshippers

For lay worshippers, the church's message centers on two themes: the proper management of death (funeral rites conducted correctly, the dead treated with dignity, inheritance and property handled according to law) and the proper relationship to magical talent in oneself and one's community (if you have magical ability, you are obligated to develop it; if you don't, you are obligated not to fear or persecute those who do).

In Suel communities, the church functions as the primary legal institution for managing inheritance, dispute over property of the dead, and the proper conduct of funerals. This legal function gives it significant secular authority in addition to its spiritual one.

### What the Faith Demands of the Devout

From clergy, the church demands: technical excellence in both necromantic and general magical disciplines, rigorous observance of the legal forms governing death and the dead, personal maintenance of appearance and dignity (this is explicit — slovenly clergy are considered to be failing the faith), and regular public demonstration of competence through the provision of magical and legal services to the community.

The church does not demand combat readiness in the way of martial faiths, but it does demand that its clergy be capable of protecting themselves and the institutions they serve.

## ⛪ The Clergy

### Becoming a Priest

Entry into Wee Jas's priesthood requires demonstrated magical aptitude — this is not negotiable. The church trains its clergy in both necromantic and general magical disciplines simultaneously with theology and law. Candidates are evaluated on their magical capability, their legal knowledge, and their personal presentation. A candidate who is genuinely accomplished magically but appears before the ordination committee in dirty robes with unkempt hair has failed a portion of the examination.

The church accepts women and men equally and has done so in its entire recorded history — Wee Jas herself is female, and the church makes no distinction between genders in its hierarchy or its expectations.

### Ranks & Hierarchy

- **Apprentice of the Ruby:** Trainee. Under instruction.
- **Death's Keeper:** Full initiate. Working magical-legal clergy.
- **Mortuary Advocate:** Mid-rank. Handles significant legal matters relating to the dead and manages complex necromantic operations.
- **Witch-Arbiter:** Senior clergy. Commands significant authority in both the legal and magical dimensions of the faith.
- **High Witch-Arbiter:** Regional leader. One per major Suel population center or region.
- **The Supreme Sorceress (or Sorcerer):** The church's supreme authority. Currently based in the Scarlet Brotherhood's sphere of influence; the Supreme Sorceress is the most technically accomplished magical practitioner in the faith as evaluated by a council of High Witch-Arbiters.

### Duties & Obligations

Clergy are expected to: provide funeral services and legal management of estates in their community, maintain and advance their magical skills (annual demonstration of new spell acquisition is expected above Apprentice rank), conduct the monthly Court of Law in communities under their authority, maintain personal appearance to the standard the faith considers appropriate, and report any unauthorized necromantic activity in their region to senior clergy.

### Vestments & Appearance

Blood red and black — the colors of the ruby and of death. Formal vestments are elaborately cut and immaculately maintained; the church explicitly expects its clergy to look impressive. The skull-with-ruby symbol is typically worn as jewelry (a real ruby in a silver skull setting is common for senior clergy; less expensive versions exist for lower ranks). There is no plain or modest option in this faith's vestment catalogue — every item is carefully made and well-maintained.

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The Mortuary Legion
The church's enforcement arm — warrior-clergy who specialize in the legal destruction of unauthorized undead, the enforcement of the church's legal authority over funerary matters, and the protection of the church's own necromantic operations from interference. Mortuary Legionnaires are not primarily scholars; they are trained warriors with significant necromantic capability. They are deployed when the church needs direct action rather than legal argument.

### The Court of Succession
A specialized legal institution within the church that handles inheritance and property disputes in communities where Wee Jas's law governs. The Court has no army; it has legal authority and magical capability. A ruling from the Court of Succession is binding in Suel communities and is frequently respected outside them because the Court has a deserved reputation for impartiality and thorough investigation.

### The Ruby Order
A scholarly and magical brotherhood within the church that emphasizes the development of advanced magical technique — particularly at the intersection of necromancy and general arcane magic. Ruby Order members are among the most technically sophisticated magical practitioners in the Flanaess. They share research within the Order and present findings to the church at the Night of Lawful Death annually. Access to Ruby Order research requires membership or very significant payment.

### The Jade Society
A schismatic faction within the faith — not heretical, but distinct — that places greater emphasis on the vanity portfolio and less on legal rigor. The Jade Society functions more as a magical social club for the Suel aristocratic class than as a serious religious institution, but it has significant secular influence because of the status of its members. Senior church clergy view the Jade Society with mild contempt and significant political awareness of its power.

## 🙏 Worshippers

### Primary Worshippers

- **Necromancers and death-magic specialists** who want a deity whose portfolio covers their discipline without the murder-cult baggage of Nerull.
- **Suel aristocracy** — the church is deeply embedded in the cultural and legal traditions of the Suel people.
- **Lawyers and judges** in Suel communities where the church functions as the primary legal institution for death-related matters.
- **Wizards generally** who appreciate the magical sophistication of the church's traditions even if they're not Suel.

### Secondary Worshippers

- Communities that have lost access to good-aligned death deities and need someone to perform funeral rites correctly.
- The vain and status-conscious who find the faith's attitudes about pride and presentation personally congenial.
- Those with unusual relationships to death — those who have died and been returned, those who work extensively with the dying, those who study mortality philosophically.

### Who Should NOT Approach This Faith

- Those who want to create uncontrolled undead for their own purposes. The church's position on unsanctioned necromancy is clear and enforced.
- Those who dress badly and can't be persuaded to do otherwise. This is not a joke — the church's expectations about presentation are real, and clergy who find a persistent slob asking for help will eventually decline to help them.
- Nerull worshippers. The church considers Nerull's murder theology an affront to the proper governance of death.

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

### Daily Practice

Morning ritual involves a precise, ordered preparation — the physical care of one's appearance (the faith teaches this is itself an act of worship) followed by magical study (review of current spells, advancement of ongoing research) followed by brief prayers of intention organized around the Codes of the Sorceress. Evening ritual is legal: a review of the day's conduct against the church's legal standards. Was anything done that would fail the Court of Succession's examination? If yes, the error is noted and correction planned.

### Holy Days & Festivals

**The Night of Lawful Death (autumn):** The faith's major holy night. Communities gather for a formal ceremony in which the year's dead are formally catalogued and honored — not mourned dramatically, but legally acknowledged. Estates settled, inheritances completed, and the names of the dead read aloud and added to the church's records. Major necromantic workings are conducted at the Night — the church's most significant ongoing necromantic projects are timed to this night.

**The Ruby Moon (spring):** A celebration of magical achievement and beauty. Members of the faith display work they have accomplished in the past year — magical research completed, techniques developed, beautiful objects created. The Ruby Moon has the atmosphere of an exclusive professional exhibition rather than a religious festival. Awards are given for exceptional achievement; these have genuine prestige within the Suel magical community.

**The Court of Law (monthly, full moon):** Public legal sessions in which community disputes over death-related matters are formally heard and adjudicated. The Court of Law is the church's most visible regular service to its community and is typically attended not just by Wee Jas worshippers but by anyone who has a legal matter to resolve.

### Rites of Passage

- **The Ruby Investiture:** Ordination. The new Death's Keeper demonstrates magical competence before a committee, presents their understanding of the relevant legal codes, and receives their ruby skull symbol.
- **The Advocate's Examination:** Elevation to Mortuary Advocate. A formal examination in both advanced necromantic technique and the church's full legal code, conducted orally before a panel of Witch-Arbiters.
- **The Final Accounting:** The death rite. The deceased's magical and legal work is formally reviewed; their estate is handled through the Court of Succession; their body is treated according to their own prior instructions (this is required of all clergy — pre-planning one's own death rites is considered proper form).

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### The Vault of the Dead — Hesuel Ilshar (Scarlet Brotherhood)
The church's primary sacred site — a vast underground complex beneath the Scarlet Brotherhood's heartland that has been continuously maintained for centuries. The Vault contains the embalmed remains of every Supreme Sorceress in the church's recorded history, a library of necromantic texts that is arguably the most comprehensive in the Flanaess, and the living quarters of the current Supreme Sorceress's inner circle. Access to the Vault is restricted to clergy at Witch-Arbiter level and above; the necromantic library within is accessible by invitation only.

### The Temple of the Ruby Eye — Greyhawk City
The church's largest temple outside Suel territory — a significant institution in Greyhawk's Foreign Quarter that serves the city's substantial magical community, the significant Suel diaspora population in the city, and anyone who needs legal services relating to the dead. The Temple of the Ruby Eye is one of Greyhawk's more visually striking buildings: dark stone, red glass windows, and an entrance carved to resemble the church's skull symbol at enormous scale.

### The Succession Courts — Rel Astra, Idee, and Onnwal
Three courthouses maintained by the Court of Succession in the former Great Kingdom's Suel-influenced territories. Each functions as both a temple and a legal institution. The Succession Courts have survived the political upheaval of the Great Kingdom's fall in various states of authority — Rel Astra's is flourishing under the city's independence; Idee's and Onnwal's are navigating complex post-war politics.

### The Jade Tower — Ekbir (Baklunish West)
An unexpected presence in Baklunish territory — a Jade Society establishment in Ekbir that has been maintained through several generations of Baklunish rulers who find it politically useful to have access to the church's magical and legal services. The Jade Tower is small, extremely well-funded (Jade Society membership is expensive), and maintains a kind of studied indifference to the political tensions between the Baklunish West and the Suel-heritage cultures.

## ⚖️ Divine Relations

### Allies

**Boccob:** The two most magically sophisticated divine portfolios in the Flanaess have an obvious natural alignment. The churches cooperate on magical scholarship and share some research, though Boccob's neutrality is more extensive and his church's aesthetics considerably less elegant by Wee Jas's standards.

**Hextor:** An uncomfortable but real alignment. Both are Lawful; both operate with cold pragmatism. The church cooperates with Hextor's institutions on specific legal and military matters in regions where Hextor has power and Wee Jas has legal authority over the dead.

### Rivals & Neutral Relations

**Nerull:** The theological opposition here is genuine — Nerull's murder of the living is a violation of the proper order of death, which is supposed to come in its time and through correct process. The churches are not at war but are not allies. Wee Jas's clergy will oppose Nerullian operations where they affect the church's jurisdiction over the dead.

**Heironeous and the good-aligned death-adjacent clergy:** The church's neutrality positions it awkwardly against the good-aligned faithful who want clear moral commitments.

### Enemies

**Nerull:** See above — the primary theological opposition.

**Iuz:** The cambion's disrespect for any law or order, including the law of death, makes him an enemy. His use of undead without proper governance is, to the church, a specific legal violation.

## 🎲 Player's Guide to This Faith

### Seeking Help From This Church

Wee Jas's church is useful in very specific ways: magical identification and consultation (excellent), death-related legal services (extraordinary — no better resource in the Flanaess for figuring out who inherited what from a dead wizard), access to necromantic knowledge that would be unavailable through good-aligned faiths.

The church is transactional and professional. It provides services for appropriate payment, maintains excellent records, and keeps its commitments. It does not ask whether you're a good person. It does ask whether you're properly attired for the meeting.

### What the Church Wants From Adventurers

- **Recovery of magical texts**, particularly Suel-era magical manuscripts and necromantic texts from ruins.
- **Legal investigation** — tracking down heirs to estates, recovering stolen property of the dead.
- **Destruction of unauthorized undead** — the church pays for this, which surprises people. Uncontrolled undead are a violation of the church's legal territory.
- **Protection of Court of Succession activities** in politically unstable regions.

### How to Earn Favor

- Bring the church magical knowledge it doesn't have.
- Dress appropriately when meeting its clergy. This is not metaphorical advice.
- Report unauthorized necromantic activity to the nearest Death's Keeper.
- Respect the church's authority over death-related legal matters even when it's inconvenient.

### How to Lose Favor (Red Flags)

- Creating undead without the church's sanction in regions where the church has authority. They will find out, and they will have opinions.
- Treating the church's legal formalities as obstacles to be bypassed. The formalities are the point.
- Disrespecting the dead in ways that violate the church's legal codes — looting graves indiscriminately, disposing of bodies without proper rites in Suel communities.

### Clerical Advice (If Playing a Priest of This Deity)

The Wee Jas priest is an intellectually sophisticated character archetype: a magical-legal practitioner with significant necromantic capability who operates from a position of principled neutrality rather than good or evil. The vanity portfolio is often overlooked, but it's one of the more interesting roleplaying elements — your character genuinely believes that taking care of their appearance and taking pride in their achievements is a form of worship, and this creates a distinctive and slightly unsettling confidence.

The Death domain combined with the Arcana domain (or access to the Magic domain in older editions) makes for a powerful but not combat-focused character. You are the party's magical expert and legal mind; you are not their front-line fighter, and you know it.
