## 🌟 Overview

Hanseath is the dwarven god of the feast and the fight — and specifically the moment when the two become indistinguishable, which is the theological core of his faith. He is the patron of dwarven berserkers, those warriors who enter battle in a state of controlled or uncontrolled abandon, and of the ale-culture that lubricates dwarven social life in its more raucous expressions. He is the least dignified of the major dwarven deities, and he is fine with this.

He appears as an enormous dwarf with an extravagantly large beard (always partially damp with ale), a battleaxe in one hand and a tankard in the other, with an expression of pure, uncomplicated joy. He is in the middle of both fighting and feasting simultaneously, and he sees no reason to stop either. His theology is the simplest in the dwarven pantheon: the feast and the battle are the same thing, both expressions of the life-force at its most direct, and both worthy of divine celebration.

His relationship with the rest of the dwarven pantheon is warm but slightly embarrassing — Moradin finds him excessive; Dumathoin finds him loud; Vergadain finds him bad for business. Hanseath does not particularly care about any of this.

---

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

1. **Feast and fight are the same prayer.** The faith's central claim is that the full expression of the dwarven life-force is visible in both the battle and the feast — both are about total engagement, total presence, total expenditure of self in the moment. The berserker in battle and the dwarf at the table after it are doing the same thing.
2. **Hold nothing back.** The berserker tradition that Hanseath governs is specifically about the abandonment of self-preservation in the service of total engagement. This is not recklessness — the experienced berserker knows how to enter and exit the state — but it is the deliberate release of caution in the service of something larger.
3. **Share the ale.** The communal dimension of the faith is expressed in the obligation to share — the feast is not a feast if it is eaten alone, and the battle is not celebrated if the celebration is private. Hanseath's faith is explicitly communal in its most important expressions.

## 🎲 Player's Guide

Hanseath's faith is the most accessible dwarven faith for non-dwarves, because the common elements of his portfolio — drinking, fighting, celebrating — are universally legible. His clergy are encountered at significant dwarven celebrations and in the quarters of berserker warriors. A Hanseath dwarf is a character of extraordinary physical confidence and social warmth — someone who meets the world at full engagement in both its dangerous and its pleasurable expressions.
