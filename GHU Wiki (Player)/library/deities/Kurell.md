## 🌟 Overview

Kurell is the Oeridian god of jealousy — the emotion, the theft it motivates, and the revenge it demands. He is not a comfortable deity to worship, and the faith makes no effort to make him more comfortable than the emotion he represents. He is the god of the person who looks at what someone else has and feels an anger that goes beyond desire — who feels that the other person's possession is itself a wrong, that the universe is unfair in its distribution, and who intends to correct this personally.

He is the younger brother of Zilchus (the god of power and wealth) in the Oeridian pantheon — and this sibling relationship is one of the most psychologically rich in the divine mythology. Kurell is what Zilchus's success looks like from the outside to those who did not achieve it: the god of those who feel the game was rigged, who feel they were cheated, who feel the successful person's success came at their expense. This is sometimes accurate and sometimes not, but Kurell does not evaluate the accuracy of the grievance. He honors the feeling.

He appears as a lean, dark-eyed man with a constant sideways gaze — always looking at what someone else has. The broken coin in his symbol is the deal that went wrong, the partnership that cheated him, the inheritance that was stolen. Kurell's mythology is full of things that were his and are now not his, and that he intends to recover.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

1. **What was taken can be taken back.** The faith's central claim is that theft and revenge are symmetrical — if you were robbed, you are owed. This extends from literal theft to the vaguer category of things that "should have been" yours: the promotion, the relationship, the recognition. Kurell's theology provides divine legitimacy to the conviction that the universe owes you something.
2. **Jealousy is honest.** The faith teaches that jealousy is the emotion that tells you what you actually value, stripped of social performance. Where you feel jealousy, you understand what you truly want. Kurell's worshippers are encouraged to honor the emotion rather than suppress it, though not necessarily to act on it immediately or without planning.
3. **Never forgive without restitution.** The faith is explicitly opposed to the unconditional forgiveness traditions of good-aligned faiths (Zodal in particular). Forgiveness without restitution is, in Kurell's theology, the demand that the harmed party absorb the wrong themselves — an additional injustice on top of the original one.

## ⛪ The Clergy

Kurell's clergy are the least institutional in the Oeridian pantheon — there are no great temples, no organized hierarchy, no regularized training program. The faith organizes around individual priests who accumulate worshippers with similar grievances, provide ritualistic framing for revenge operations, and serve as spiritual counselors for the resentful. Some function as fence operations for stolen goods, providing theological legitimacy for theft in exchange for a cut.

The clergy are not evil by nature — Kurell is Chaotic Neutral, not evil — but the faith's tolerance for revenge and theft means that its clergy attract people on the edge of more explicitly harmful activity, and maintaining the distinction between legitimate grievance and predatory resentment is an ongoing challenge that the faith largely fails to meet.

---

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

**The Night of the Broken Coin:** The faith's primary observance — a night on which old grievances are formally rehearsed in a ritual setting. Worshippers name what was taken from them, name who took it, and commit to the appropriate response. The clergy preside over this naming, which is understood to have theological weight — Kurell witnesses the grievance and endorses the response. The Night's tone varies enormously by community: in some it is a solemn occasion for genuine processing of legitimate wrongs; in others it is an excuse for cataloguing petty resentments and planning revenge.

---

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The Hand's Return
The faith's loose network of revenge operatives — thieves and assassins who work under Kurell's theological mandate, specializing in recovering things that were "wrongfully" taken. The Hand's Return does not evaluate the legitimacy of the client's claim; they accept the client's version and proceed accordingly. This makes them dangerous to anyone who has made an enemy of someone with Kurell connections. They are more expensive than secular thieves but provide a ritual framing that gives the client — and sometimes the operative — psychological satisfaction that the merely mercenary approach does not.

## 🎲 Player's Guide

Kurell's church is the resource for: channeling revenge into something productive, finding operatives willing to steal back something stolen from you, and accessing the social network of the grievance community — which tends to have detailed intelligence on specific individuals who have wronged its members. The faith's moral framework is genuinely complicated: some of its worshippers have entirely legitimate grievances and are seeking entirely proportionate responses; others are nursing resentments that have grown well past the original wrong. A Kurell character is one of the most psychologically interesting in the game — someone whose faith gives theological weight to an emotion that most good-aligned traditions dismiss or condemn, and who must decide what they actually do with that.
