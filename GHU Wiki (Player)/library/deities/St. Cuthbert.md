## 🌟 Overview

St. Cuthbert occupies a peculiar and beloved position among the gods of Oerth: he is the deity of common sense, of practical wisdom, of the kind of blunt-force honesty that refuses to dress itself up in doctrine. He was, by some accounts, a mortal man before his ascension — a preacher of uncommon directness who carried a wooden cudgel, spoke plainly to anyone who would listen, and knocked some sense into those who wouldn't. Whether or not the literal story is true, the characterization is accurate. Cuthbert is the god who will look you in the face and tell you that you are making a mistake — and who may demonstrate this point with a mace if necessary.

His alignment sits at a peculiar intersection: he is Lawful Good, but his "good" is frequently expressed through a fierce, almost exasperated enforcement of right behavior. He has little patience for evil and even less patience for stupidity and self-deception that leads to evil. His church has been described by its critics as "warmly authoritarian" — they genuinely care about people's wellbeing, but they can be relentless in pushing people toward better choices. The faith's greatest internal tension is between its genuine compassion and its tendency to assume it knows best.

St. Cuthbert is not a sophisticated deity. He does not deal in mystery, prophecy, or theological abstraction. He deals in clear rules, consistent enforcement, and the practical results of honesty versus deception. This makes his faith deeply accessible to common people — farmers, laborers, merchants, guards — who find the complexity of other religions bewildering and the directness of Cuthbert's message refreshing.

He is one of the most politically powerful religious institutions in the Flanaess, with a significant presence in the Kingdom of Furyondy, the Shield Lands, and the Wild Coast. His church functions as one of the most reliable sources of justice in regions where secular legal institutions have failed.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

### The Fundamental Tenets

The church has five principles it calls the Cudgel's Laws. Every initiate learns them before their second week:

1. **Common sense before piety.** An elaborate religious ceremony that ignores an obvious practical problem is worthless. God is not impressed by ceremony; God is impressed by results.
2. **Honesty, even when it costs you.** This is not merely a prohibition against lying. It is an affirmative command to say true things, especially when those true things are unwelcome.
3. **Discipline your impulses.** Anger, lust, greed, fear — these are natural. A person of good character does not eliminate them but governs them. The cudgel disciplines; it does not destroy.
4. **Hold others to their word.** When someone makes a vow, promise, or commitment, they have made a claim about their character. Allowing them to abandon it without consequence is a disservice to them.
5. **Enforce justice without venom.** The church distinguishes between punishment, which is just and necessary, and cruelty, which is not. A criminal who is appropriately punished has been served; a criminal who is humiliated and tortured has been wronged. St. Cuthbert's justice is firm but not sadistic.

### What the Faith Preaches to Ordinary Worshippers

The church's message to common people is practical: keep your promises, tell the truth, think before you act, and don't let clever people talk you out of knowing what you know. The last point is important — Cuthbert's faith emphasizes that ordinary intelligence and ordinary experience produce genuine wisdom, and that this wisdom should not be steamrolled by educated sophistication.

The church actively encourages its congregation to push back when authorities (including the church itself) say things that contradict common experience. This creates occasional awkwardness when church doctrine and practical reality diverge, but it also makes the faith genuinely honest in a way that more doctrinally rigid religions are not.

### What the Faith Demands of the Devout

- Keep every vow, promise, and commitment you make, regardless of inconvenience.
- Report dishonesty when you observe it. The church maintains a network of informants — not for political espionage, but for tracking fraud, oath-breaking, and systematic deception in communities.
- Submit to the church's discipline when you fail. Cuthbert's faith practices formal accountability: members who break their own standards are expected to acknowledge this and accept appropriate penance. This is not punishment for its own sake but a mechanism for re-establishing integrity.
- Carry a cudgel, a billet, or a mace. This is optional for lay members but expected for full initiates — it is a symbol of readiness to enforce right behavior when words fail.

## ⛪ The Clergy

### Becoming a Priest

Cuthbert's church accepts initiates from any background. The vetting process is unusually rigorous — not theologically, but character-wise. Prospective initiates are observed for six months before formal training begins. The church wants to know if they are honest in ordinary circumstances, not just when observed or tested. Priests who pass through training and are later found to have been dishonest during the vetting period are expelled, regardless of their subsequent service record.

Training emphasizes law, practical reasoning, dispute resolution, and the history of the faith. Combat training is mandatory — even non-combat clergy are expected to be competent with a mace. The church produces effective fighters because it expects them to do physical work, including enforcement.

### Ranks & Hierarchy

- **Chapeaux (Postulant):** Named for the flat-brimmed hat new initiates wear. Chapeaux are in training and observation.
- **Canoness / Canon:** Full priest. Responsible for a congregation.
- **Curate:** Supervises multiple Canons in a district.
- **Vicar:** Regional leader, typically operating from a major town or city temple.
- **Proctor:** Equivalent to a bishop; oversees entire provinces.
- **The Patriarch / Matriarch:** The supreme authority of the church. Currently seated in Verbobonc.

### Duties & Obligations

The church's primary civic function is legal mediation. Where secular courts are weak, corrupt, or absent, Cuthbert's clergy serve as arbiters of disputes, mediators in contracts, and enforcers of community standards. This is not volunteer work — the church formally charges for legal mediation services, and the resulting income is one reason the faith is so financially stable.

Clergy are also expected to perform regular street ministry — not preaching, but presence. Sitting in a public place, being visibly available for questions, conversations, and disputes. The church has found that visible, accessible clergy prevent more problems than secretive ones.

### Vestments & Appearance

Brown and red robes are the standard clerical colors. The brown represents common earth and ordinary things; the red represents the ruby starburst of the holy symbol. Senior clergy add a starburst embroidered in red thread. The distinctive billet — a short wooden cudgel — hangs at every priest's belt, regardless of what other weapon they carry. It is worn as conspicuously as the holy symbol and is understood to be both a tool and a symbol.

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The Chapeaux (General Congregation)

Not technically a sect, but the Chapeaux designation applies to the church's full lay member community, which is unusually organized for a Flanaessian religion. Lay Chapeaux maintain registers of their vows and submit annual accounting of kept and broken promises to their local Canon.

### The Billets (Enforcement Order)

The Billets are the church's enforcement arm: trained fighters and paladins who specialize in tracking oath-breakers, retrieving those who have fled legal judgments, and dismantling criminal organizations that operate through systematic deception. They are not assassins — they arrest, they don't kill — but they are persistent and effective. In regions without strong secular law enforcement, Billets fill the gap.

### The Wandering Episcopate

A tradition rather than a formal order: senior clergy who have retired from administrative roles and travel the roads, ministering to rural communities and resolving disputes on an itinerant basis. Wandering Episcopate members are highly respected and their judgments are considered binding in areas where no formal secular authority exists.

### The Stern Reapers (Schismatic Sub-sect)

A controversial internal movement in the church's Lawful Neutral wing. The Stern Reapers believe that St. Cuthbert's mercy is too generous — that persistent wrongdoers who repeatedly fail to reform should be expelled from community or killed, not perpetually rehabilitated. Mainstream church leadership considers the Reapers dangerous extremists; the Reapers consider mainstream leadership dangerously naive. This tension is managed rather than resolved.

## 🙏 Worshippers

### Primary Worshippers

- **Common folk of all classes.** Cuthbert's faith is, more than almost any other Flanaessian religion, a religion of ordinary people. Farmers, laborers, craftsmen, and small merchants make up the bulk of his worshippers.
- **Judges, magistrates, and lawyers** who take their oaths of office seriously.
- **Town guards and militia.** The church's emphasis on practical discipline and community protection appeals to those who protect communities without magical resources.
- **Fighters and warriors** who value discipline over inspiration.

### Secondary Worshippers

- Anyone who has been seriously wronged by broken promises or fraud. The church of Cuthbert has a reputation for actually helping with these cases.
- People who find other religions too mystical, too elaborate, or too focused on theological abstraction rather than practical living.
- Communities in regions where secular law is absent or corrupt.

### Who Should NOT Approach This Faith

- Habitual liars. They will be identified and ejected — possibly physically.
- Those seeking to use the church's legal authority for personal vendettas rather than genuine justice. The clergy are trained to distinguish these cases.
- Worshippers of Olidammara (the god of trickery and rogues). The two faiths are not violently opposed, but they are profoundly philosophically incompatible, and Cuthbert's clergy are suspicious of anyone with that particular alignment.

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

### Daily Practice

Morning and evening prayers are short and practical: a statement of the day's commitments in the morning, an honest accounting of how they were kept or broken in the evening. The accounting is not punitive — it is reflective. Priests teach that the goal is not a perfect record but an honest one.

### Holy Days & Festivals

**Growfest — The Cudgel's Week:** Seven days in early spring when the church focuses on the enforcement of outstanding vows. Contracts that expired unfulfilled are reviewed. Oaths made in the previous year are formally renewed. It is not a celebratory festival — it is more like an annual audit, taken seriously.

**Richfest — The Honesty Day:** A single day at midsummer. The church encourages its members to have one genuinely honest conversation they have been avoiding. Confessions, difficult truths, long-withheld apologies — these are considered sacred acts this day. The church records an astonishing number of resolved disputes and restored relationships each Richfest.

**The Reckoning (Autumnal Equinox):** The year's most solemn observance. Every member of the faith, from Patriarch to newest Chapeaux, stands before their congregation and states publicly what they vowed to do in the previous year and what they actually did. Failed vows are acknowledged; extraordinary service is recognized. The Reckoning takes hours in large congregations and is considered the church's most sacred ceremony.

### Rites of Passage

- **The Vow of Right Conduct:** Made when a child comes of age. A formal statement of personal commitments — not a religious vow to Cuthbert specifically, but a commitment to live honestly. The church witnesses it and keeps a record.
- **The Marriage Bond:** St. Cuthbert's marriage ceremonies are legally binding in virtually every jurisdiction in the Flanaess — his church's record-keeping and oath-witnessing authority is recognized by most secular courts.
- **The Final Accounting:** A deathbed rite. The dying person is asked to account for their vows — which they kept, which they failed. The priest offers blessing and ensures any outstanding obligations are formally transferred or released.

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### The Grand Temple — Verbobonc
The seat of the Patriarch and the church's administrative center. The Grand Temple of Verbobonc is a large but architecturally austere building — Cuthbert's faith does not spend money on decoration when it could spend money on function. Inside, it houses the church's most comprehensive legal archives: centuries of contracts, oaths, judgments, and dispute records. Legal scholars travel from across the Flanaess to consult these archives. The church charges for access, and the fee is not small.

### The Temple of the Cudgel — City of Greyhawk
The church's primary urban presence in the Free City. Positioned in the Artisan's Quarter, it serves primarily as a mediation and legal services center. The clergy here are among the best-trained dispute mediators in the Flanaess, and their services are in constant demand. The temple maintains a vigorous Billets chapter that has worked with City Watch to prosecute fraud cases.

### The Shield Temple — Shield Lands
Built in the period when the Shield Lands were actively fighting against Iuz, this temple served as a center for oath-taking among the Shield Lands military. Soldiers swore their war oaths here. When the Shield Lands fell, the temple was desecrated but not destroyed — Iuz's forces found it more useful to corrupt the building than demolish it. Restoration is an ongoing project and a matter of significant theological importance to the faith.

### The House of Common Judgment — Dyvers
A temple that doubles as a courthouse. The Dyvers city government has a formal arrangement with the church of St. Cuthbert: the church provides legally binding arbitration services as a branch of the civic judiciary, in exchange for the city's recognition of the church's authority to maintain its own internal legal processes. It is an unusual and much-studied arrangement.

### Roadside Cudgel Shrines
St. Cuthbert's roadside shrines are distinctive: a simple wooden post with a cudgel carved into it at crossroads and village commons. They are far less elaborate than Pelor's sun-disk shrines but just as ubiquitous. Local communities sometimes add carved notes of public oaths to the post — a form of public commitment-keeping the church encourages.

## ⚖️ Divine Relations

### Allies

**Heironeous:** Cuthbert and Heironeous are allied — both are Good-aligned, law-oriented deities who oppose evil through organized force. Their churches cooperate frequently, though they have theological disagreements about the role of mercy in justice (Cuthbert's church is slightly more merciful than Heironeous's in practice).

**Rao:** Despite Rao's pacifism and Cuthbert's vigorous enforcement approach, the two faiths align on their fundamental commitment to truth and right conduct. Rao's church respects Cuthbert's honesty; Cuthbert's church respects Rao's principled consistency.

### Rivals & Neutral Relations

**Pelor:** Friendly but different in emphasis. Pelor heals; Cuthbert corrects. Both are needed; neither is wrong.

**Wee Jas:** Both are Lawful, but Wee Jas's alignment with death and her aristocratic aesthetic puts her at odds with Cuthbert's populist directness. Their churches don't fight, but they don't cooperate well either.

### Enemies

**Iuz:** The cambion's empire of deceit and pain is everything Cuthbert opposes. His church actively funds and organizes resistance to Iuz in the north.

**Olidammara:** Not enemies in the violent sense, but profound philosophical opponents. Olidammara is the god of tricks and deception; Cuthbert is the god of honesty and common sense. Their clergy aggravate each other intensely.

**Nerull and Hextor:** Standard enemies for a Good-aligned church.

## 🎲 Player's Guide to This Faith

### Seeking Help From This Church

Cuthbert's church is extremely helpful if you are honest with them and moderately unhelpful if you aren't. Walk in, state your situation plainly, and ask for what you need. The clergy are trained to identify fabrication and will call it out immediately — not angrily, but directly. If you are truthful, they will treat you fairly.

The church will help with: legal disputes, contracts and oaths, healing (they have clerical ability but it is not their primary ministry), identifying fraud and deception, tracking down oath-breakers, and in some cases providing trained Billets as escort or backup.

### What the Church Wants From Adventurers

- **Enforcement work.** Tracking down those who have fled legal judgments — fugitives from the church's mediation authority.
- **Recovery of stolen records** — the church's legal archives are occasionally targeted by those who want to destroy evidence.
- **Dismantling deception rings.** Organized fraud, con operations, or systematic oath-breaking.
- **Testimony.** The church occasionally needs reliable witnesses for legal proceedings and pays well for trustworthy sworn statements.

### How to Earn Favor

- Be conspicuously honest, especially about yourself. Admitting a mistake earns more goodwill in this church than explaining why you didn't make one.
- Keep vows made before the church, even inconvenient ones.
- Bring evidence of systematic deception or oath-breaking to clergy attention.
- Perform enforcement work (Billets missions) efficiently and without unnecessary cruelty.

### How to Lose Favor (Red Flags)

- Being caught in a lie, especially a small one. The church watches for habitual small deceits.
- Using the church's legal authority to pursue personal enemies rather than genuine justice.
- Breaking an oath made before the church.
- Being associated with Olidammara's church — guilt by association is a risk.

### Clerical Advice (If Playing a Priest of This Deity)

The mace is not optional — it is your symbol as much as your holy symbol. Carry it visibly. Know the Cudgel's Laws well enough to cite them in conversation without thinking about it. The church expects its clerics to function as mobile magistrates: wherever you are, you are an authority on disputes, oaths, and right conduct, and people will bring you problems. Be ready for this.

Roleplay the evening accounting when possible. At the end of the day, your character notes what they said they would do and what they actually did. It doesn't have to be elaborate — even a brief in-character acknowledgment ("I said I would deal with the gate guard today; I did") grounds the character in the faith's discipline.
