## 🌟 Overview

Beory is the World Mother — the goddess who is, in the most literal theological sense her church teaches, the planet Oerth itself. Not a goddess who governs the planet from above; a goddess who is the planet, whose body is the soil, whose breath is the wind, whose blood is the water, whose heartbeat is the seasonal cycle. This is not metaphor in Beory's theology. When her worshippers plant seeds in the spring, they are touching the goddess directly. When it rains, the goddess is giving of herself. When the harvest fails, it is not the goddess's punishment — it is the goddess in distress.

Her symbol is the simplest of any deity's in the Flanaess: a green disk. No ornamentation, no instrument, no weapon. The circle of the world, in the color of growing things.

Beory is ancient. She predates the organized human cultures of the Flanaess — the Flan people's oldest religious traditions involve her, and some scholars of ancient Oerth believe she predates even them, that the very first humans on Oerth immediately sensed the world's consciousness and named what they sensed. Whether this is theology or history is a question the church considers a false distinction.

She is neutral in the truest sense — she is the world, and the world sustains both good and evil without preference. This is the aspect of Beory's theology that most challenges good-aligned worshippers who find her: the rain falls on the righteous and the wicked alike, because rain is not a moral instrument. Crops grow for farmers who worship evil gods as readily as for those who worship good ones. Beory's neutrality is not philosophical; it is natural. The world simply is.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

### The Fundamental Tenets

Beory's faith is organized around the cycle — the most fundamental pattern of existence. The Three Truths of the Cycle:

1. **All things return to the earth.** Death is not an ending; it is a return. What dies feeds what lives; what is taken from the earth is returned to the earth. The cycle cannot be broken, only disrupted — and disruption is always temporary and always painful.
2. **The earth sustains; the earth requires sustenance.** The relationship between farmers and the land is a relationship of mutual obligation. Take from the earth, but return to it. Plant, harvest, fallow, plant again. Exhaust the land and it will exhaust you.
3. **The seasons cannot be hurried.** What grows, grows in its time. What rests, rests for a reason. The desire to force growth out of season, to demand that the earth produce beyond its natural capacity, is a form of hubris that the earth corrects in its own time.

### For Ordinary Worshippers

The church's message to agricultural communities is profoundly practical: treat the land as a living partner, observe the seasonal cycle in your practices, take care of the soil, and the soil will take care of you. The faith provides a theological framework for sustainable agriculture that communities have found effective across generations.

Beory's faith is also one of the few that directly addresses community grief — in its understanding that death is not an ending but a return, it provides comfort that most agricultural communities find genuinely helpful rather than philosophically demanding.

## ⛪ The Clergy

Beory's clergy are primarily druids organized around land stewardship. They are farmers as much as priests — they maintain sacred fields, keep seasonal ritual calendars, provide advice on agricultural practices, and perform the blessing ceremonies that the communities they serve consider essential to successful harvests. The clergy's hierarchy is informal and based on experience and demonstrated connection to the land rather than formal rank.

The vestments are earth-tone undyed fabrics, green in spring and summer, brown and gold in autumn, grey in winter. No elaboration — the simplicity is itself a statement about the faith's values.

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

**Planting Day (spring):** The blessing of seeds and the first formal turning of soil for the planting season. The ceremony involves prayers, the formal marking of the field's boundaries, and the symbolic first planting by the priest before the community's own planting begins. Beory is directly petitioned to sustain the seeds and bring rain in its proper season.

**Harvest Home (autumn):** A thanksgiving ceremony at harvest completion. The first sheaves of grain are brought to the shrine; prayers of gratitude are offered; the community shares a meal from the harvest's produce. It is one of the warmest holy days in the agricultural tradition.

**The Rain Festival:** Not a fixed holy day but a ceremonial response to drought. When rain fails at critical points in the growing season, communities gather for formal petitions to Beory — not demanding rain, but acknowledging distress and asking the World Mother's attention.

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### The World's Navel — Central Flanaess
A location identified by Beory's most ancient traditions as the point where the World Mother's consciousness is most accessible — a specific geographical location in the central Flanaess that is maintained as a sacred site by the faith's senior druids. The World's Navel is not publicly identified; its location is passed from senior druid to successor.

### Sacred Fields
Specific agricultural areas maintained by the church in perpetuity — not farming for production but farming as continuous ritual practice. Sacred Fields are maintained by grove-keeping traditions in ways that demonstrate the proper human relationship to the earth. They are accessible to visitors and are used for ceremonial plantings at holy days.

## ⚖️ Divine Relations

**Allies:** Obad-Hai (she is the planet; he is her forests; the relationship is fundamental), Pelor (the Sun Father's warmth makes the World Mother's agriculture possible), Ehlonna.

**Neutral toward all:** Beory's neutrality is the most complete of any deity's. She has no enemies because she is the ground enemies and friends alike walk on.

## 🎲 Player's Guide to This Faith

Beory's church is most accessible to agricultural communities as a resource for farming advice, seasonal ceremonies, and healing. It is less useful to adventurers except in specific contexts: finding safe shelter in wilderness areas where Beory's druids are active, understanding the agricultural condition of regions you're traveling through, or dealing with ecological damage that has spiritual dimensions. The church will treat honestly with anyone who approaches respectfully, regardless of alignment — the World Mother sustains all things, and her clergy reflect this impartiality.
