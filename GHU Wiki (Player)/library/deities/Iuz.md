## 🌟 Overview

Iuz the Old is not a distant, abstract divine power. He is a person — a being who was born, who made choices, who walked the streets of the Flanaess, who was imprisoned, who escaped, and who has spent the better part of two centuries building an empire of cruelty across the northern Flanaess. His demigod status is the result of accumulated divine power through worship and through his nature as the cambion son of Zuggtmoy (the demon queen of fungi) and the human archmage Iggwilv — a being who began with divine potential and pursued it through conquest.

He appears in his own territory in physical form: a lean, ancient-seeming figure who alternates between an apparently old man's body and a monstrous, demonic form that reflects his true nature. He can choose which to show. His face — the grinning skull of his symbol, but also his actual face when he chooses to display his demonic aspects — is one of the most recognized and most feared images in the northern Flanaess.

Iuz's portfolio — deceit, pain, oppression, evil — is less a coherent theological vision than an accurate description of his nature and his preferred methods. He does not represent evil because he has a theology about it; he represents evil because he is evil, and his divine power is the projection of his personal nature onto the divine plane. This makes him, in some ways, the purest expression of evil in the pantheon: not an abstraction but a presence, not a principle but a will.

His territory — the Empire of Iuz — covers a vast swath of the northern Flanaess centered on the region once called the Land of Iuz. It includes conquered populations of multiple races, a supernatural architecture of oppression maintained by both magic and mundane military force, and a religious structure that is less a church than a personal cult. Iuz does not want worshippers who follow an institution; he wants followers who fear and serve him personally.

The freed demonic hordes of Zuggtmoy, parts of the Horned Society's former membership, the Boneheart (his inner council of powerful wizards and clerics), numerous evil humanoid warlords, and the remnants of the Bandit Kingdoms' criminal elite all form the fabric of his power structure. It is not stable; it is held together by Iuz's direct power and by the fear of what happens to those who disappoint him.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

### The Fundamental Tenets

Iuz's "church" does not have a coherent theological tradition because Iuz does not want one. Institutions develop their own interests; Iuz wants his servants loyal to him personally, not to an institution that might survive without him. What passes for Iuz's dogma is a set of operational expectations:

1. **Iuz is the only power that matters.** The faith does not acknowledge other gods except as obstacles to be overcome or as sources of power that can be stolen. Iuz's clerics who worship other gods are traitors; Iuz's clerics who use other gods' power while subjugating their worship to Iuz are acceptable.
2. **Suffering is proof of Iuz's power.** The faith celebrates pain not for its own sake but as a demonstration of Iuz's authority: he can make people suffer, and he does, therefore he is powerful, therefore he should be served.
3. **Obedience or death.** The faith has no middle category. You serve Iuz or you are an enemy. Enemies are punished as examples.
4. **Expansion is continuous.** Iuz's territorial ambition has no defined limit. His faith is explicitly committed to the ongoing conquest of everything beyond his current borders.

### What the Faith Preaches to Ordinary Worshippers

In Iuz's territory, "ordinary worship" is coercive. The populations under his control perform the required observances because the alternative has been made graphically clear. Genuine religious sentiment among these populations is uncommon and dangerous to express — it implies a primary loyalty other than to Iuz, which is suspicious.

Outside his territory, Iuz's cult operates in secret and attracts those who see his power as evidence that alignment with him offers personal advantage. These external cultists are treated by Iuz's church as assets to be exploited rather than members to be cared for.

### What the Faith Demands of the Devout

From his inner circle (the Boneheart and the various warlords and cult leaders who serve him directly), Iuz demands demonstrated loyalty, demonstrated effectiveness, and demonstrated willingness to act on his behalf regardless of personal cost. He does not forgive failure in his inner circle because failure is evidence of insufficient commitment.

From the mass of his clergy, he demands obedience, tribute, and the ongoing maintenance of suffering in whatever population they govern.

## ⛪ The Clergy

### Becoming a Priest

Iuz's priests are not selected through any normal church process. They rise through survival in his service — those who demonstrate genuine power, genuine ruthlessness, and genuine loyalty to Iuz personally are elevated. There is no seminary, no theological examination. There is performance review by Iuz or his Boneheart, and the only review metric that matters is whether you are useful.

### Ranks & Hierarchy

Iuz's structure is deliberately informal to prevent any institution from developing that could function without his direct oversight. In practice:

- **Cultist:** Someone who has sworn personal loyalty to Iuz and been accepted into his service.
- **Witch-Priest / Warlord-Priest:** A cultist who has developed genuine divine power and commands a contingent of followers.
- **Herald of Iuz:** A senior servant with significant military or magical power who commands a territory or a major operation.
- **The Boneheart:** The inner council — approximately a dozen of the most powerful wizards, clerics, and sorcerers in Iuz's service, who govern his empire in his absence and advise him on major decisions. The Boneheart is divided into the Greater Boneheart (seven members of extraordinary power) and the Lesser Boneheart (five members of significant but lesser power).
- **Iuz Himself:** Personal demigod authority. He does not delegate final decisions on anything he considers important.

### Duties & Obligations

Iuz's clergy maintain order (such as it is) in his territory, conduct the required holy day observances, and serve as the magical and supernatural backbone of his military force. The most significant clerical function in Iuz's empire is the maintenance of the undead legions — massive forces of animated dead that supplement his humanoid armies.

### Vestments & Appearance

The faith has no consistent vestment tradition — Iuz's servants wear what serves their function. Some have adopted the grinning skull symbol; some display his actual face in painted iconography; some display nothing and rely on reputation alone. The lack of consistent appearance is partly intentional — distinguishing genuine Iuz cultists from those impersonating them is something Iuz occasionally finds useful.

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The Boneheart
Covered above — the inner council is the closest thing to a formal institution in Iuz's power structure. Its members are known and feared across the northern Flanaess. Killing a Boneheart member is one of the most significant things an adventuring party could accomplish, and one of the most dangerous things it could attempt.

### The Boneshadow
An intelligence and assassination organization within Iuz's service — not formally connected to the religious structure but operating under his authority. The Boneshadow handles operations that require deniability or precision that his conventional military forces can't provide.

### The Chaos Machine
Less an organization than a function: Iuz maintains agents specifically tasked with destabilizing regions he intends to conquer — introducing factions, assassinating leaders, spreading damaging rumors, and generally creating the conditions of chaos that make organized defense difficult. The Chaos Machine's agents are not always cultists; they are sometimes simply ambitious people who have been given resources in exchange for specific destabilization work.

## 🙏 Worshippers

### Primary Worshippers

- **Humanoids under his dominion** — orcs, hobgoblins, gnolls, and others who fight under his banner because they are in his territory and the alternative is death.
- **Human populations in his territory** who have been coerced into worship.
- **Genuine cultists** who have chosen to align with him for personal advantage or ideological affinity.

### Secondary Worshippers

- External cultists in the Bandit Kingdoms remnants, the Wild Coast, and other politically unstable regions who are hedging their bets.
- Individual evil practitioners who want access to his power without formal service.

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

### Holy Days & Festivals

**The Day of Pain (midsummer):** Mass public spectacles of suffering — executions, torture demonstrations, and combat in his arena-temples — that serve as both religious observance and practical demonstration of his power to controlled populations. The Day of Pain is also when his most senior clerics conduct the year's most significant necromantic workings, adding major undead to his legions.

**The Feast of Iuz (winter solstice):** A night of violence, excess, and debauchery in his territories — partially a genuine religious observance for his inner cultists, partially an institutionalized permission structure that lets his humanoid armies blow off steam in controlled ways. The Feast is also when Iuz is most likely to appear personally in his territory.

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### Dorakaa — Capital of the Empire
The primary city of Iuz's empire — a large, supernaturally dark settlement built from the ruins of previous communities. Dorakaa is maintained in a state of controlled squalor: impressive enough to be intimidating, degraded enough to be humiliating to those forced to live there. The central palace is Iuz's personal seat when he is in residence. It is heavily warded and defended by his most powerful servants.

### The Howling Hills — Supernatural Landscape
The elevated terrain southeast of Iuz's original territory, filled with supernatural manifestations of his power — zones where the physics of the material plane are subtly wrong, where the dead don't stay dead, and where his power is concentrated. The Howling Hills is where Iuz conducts his most significant personal magical working and where some of his most dangerous servants are based.

### The Temple of the Old — Dorakaa
The only "temple" in the conventional sense associated with Iuz — a massive structure in Dorakaa's center that functions as both his primary religious site and his public throne room. The Temple of the Old is where mass sacrifices are conducted, where his clergy is formally inducted, and where petitioners (such as they are) approach with offerings.

## ⚖️ Divine Relations

### Allies

**Zuggtmoy:** His demonic mother, imprisoned in the Temple of Elemental Evil. Her prison is maintained by external forces; Iuz has not been able to free her, but he maintains her cult in her absence and considers the Zuggtmoy alliance theoretically important.

**Nerull:** Transactional. Iuz uses undead extensively; Nerull's servants harvest what Iuz's conquests produce.

**Incabulos:** Plague in conquered territories is a useful tool. Service relationship.

### Rivals

**Hextor:** The defining rivalry among evil powers. Hextor's organized-tyranny theology and Iuz's personal-power theology are competing visions of what the Flanaess could become under evil domination. They cooperate against mutual enemies but are simultaneously fighting for the same territory.

**The Scarlet Brotherhood:** An expansionist human supremacist organization whose ambitions overlap with Iuz's in the southern Flanaess.

### Enemies

**Heironeous:** The defining divine enmity from the good side. Heironeous's church has committed more resources to opposing Iuz than any other military force outside of Furyondy's secular military.

**Furyondy:** The primary secular obstacle to Iuz's expansion south.

**Rao:** The god of peace and reason represents everything Iuz disdains — he finds Rao's theology contemptible and his church's peace-negotiation work specifically threatening to his strategy.

## 🎲 Player's Guide to This Faith

### Seeking Help From This Church

You don't. There is no legitimate reason to seek help from Iuz's cult as a player character, and the attempt would be noted, reported, and used against you.

In specific circumstances — deep cover infiltration, desperate situation in his territory — knowing how his cult operates is survival information. The cult values power demonstrations and fears anything connected to the Boneheart. If you need to survive a specific encounter with Iuz's servants, appearing more valuable to them than you are threatening is the only strategy.

### What the Church Wants From Adventurers

- **Nothing positive.** Adventuring parties represent exactly the kind of unpredictable, alignment-mixed problem that Iuz's empire regularly removes.
- **Specifically:** the elimination of good-aligned champions, the removal of fortification and population centers that might resist his expansion, and the destabilization of political structures that haven't fallen yet.

### How to Lose Favor (Almost Certain)

- Interfering with any Iuz operation.
- Being associated with Heironeous, Pelor, or Furyondy.
- Demonstrating heroic capability in his territory.

### Iuz as an Antagonist

Iuz is best used by the DM not as a background threat but as an active presence — the Old whose network reaches further than expected, whose agents appear in unlikely places, whose long-term planning occasionally intersects with the party's objectives in ways that create genuine danger. His capacity to be personally present (he does appear in the material world) makes encounters with his direct power uniquely dangerous. He is one of the few villain-antagonists in the Greyhawk setting who has a defined, consistent personality that can be roleplayed — cunning, cruel, patient when patience serves him, viciously impulsive when something offends him.

The Boneheart members are excellent recurring antagonists whose distinct personalities and specializations can create a gallery of memorable villains within his power structure.
