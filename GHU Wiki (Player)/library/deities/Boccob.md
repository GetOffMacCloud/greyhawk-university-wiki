## 🌟 Overview

Boccob is the god of magic itself — not magic in service of good, not magic in service of evil, not magic as a tool of any particular ideology, but magic as a fundamental property of the universe that exists independent of morality and transcendent of alignment. He is the Uncaring: a title that his church considers more accurate than insulting. He does not favor mortals based on their goodness or wickedness. He cares about magic. He cares about arcane knowledge. He cares about the preservation and expansion of what is knowable about the magical fabric of Oerth. Everything else is secondary.

He appears in illustrations as a robed figure whose face is perpetually shadowed, carrying a staff that pulses with a light that isn't quite any color. His eye within a pentagram is one of the most recognized symbols in the Flanaess — it appears on the covers of spellbooks, above the doors of magical academies, and carved into the walls of libraries that have any connection to arcane scholarship. The symbol predates any of his churches; it appears in Flan, Baklunish, and Suel magical traditions independently, which scholars of comparative religion find either significant or coincidental and argue about at great length.

The Church of Boccob is the closest thing the magical community of the Flanaess has to a neutral institution. Other faiths pursue their gods' specific agendas; Boccob's church pursues the preservation and expansion of arcane knowledge. This makes it uniquely useful — a wizard who worships Boccob can do business with the church regardless of alignment, because the church's primary interest is in what the wizard knows, not in whether they're a good person.

This neutrality has limits. The church will not assist in the destruction of magical knowledge, will not help anyone try to unmake the magical fabric of Oerth, and has a particular horror of Tharizdun — the entity whose portfolio includes the consumption of all things, magic included. Other than that, the church maintains its careful, somewhat cold, entirely genuine uncommittedness.

## 📖 Dogma & Core Teachings

### The Fundamental Tenets

Boccob's faith is less a set of moral commandments than a set of scholarly commitments. The church organizes its doctrine around what it calls the Four Understandings:

1. **Magic is the oldest thing.** Before the gods shaped the world, before the races existed, magic was. The universe is woven from it. To study magic is to study the fundamental nature of existence.
2. **Knowledge must be preserved.** No spell, no theorem, no text of arcane scholarship should be destroyed. The church maintains archives as an act of worship, copies texts as an act of devotion, and considers the loss of any magical knowledge a genuine tragedy regardless of whether that knowledge would be used for good or evil.
3. **Balance is the natural state.** Magic flows; if it is concentrated entirely in the service of good or entirely in the service of evil, the flow is disrupted. The church does not take sides in cosmic conflicts, not because it lacks conviction but because it understands the consequences of imbalance.
4. **Foresight is the highest art.** Among all magical disciplines, divination — the ability to perceive what is, what was, and what may be — is the one Boccob is said to practice personally. The church elevates diviners and treats the cultivation of foresight as a spiritual practice as well as a scholarly one.

### What the Faith Preaches to Ordinary Worshippers

The church does not particularly pursue ordinary worshippers. It is, frankly, somewhat snobbish about membership — the faith is most accessible to those who can actually study and practice arcane magic, and its institutions are oriented toward that population. For those without arcane talent, the church offers: access to its libraries, the ability to commission magical services, and the cultural prestige of association with an institution that everyone in the magical community respects.

The simplified message to the broader public, when the church bothers: magic is not evil. Magic is a property of the universe. What matters is what you do with it. The church will not tell you what to do with it. That is your business. The church's business is making sure you know how.

### What the Faith Demands of the Devout

From its clergy, the church demands intellectual rigor above all else. A priest of Boccob who has not expanded their knowledge — not merely maintained it, but actively expanded it, through original research, recovered texts, or new experimentation — in the past year has failed in their duties. The church keeps track of its clergy's scholarly output and considers stagnation a spiritual failing.

The church also demands neutrality in practice. A cleric of Boccob who throws their institutional weight behind one side of a conflict — even a conflict between good and evil — has violated the Fourth Understanding and will be censured. Individual clerics may hold personal views; acting on them in ways that commit the church is something else entirely.

## ⛪ The Clergy

### Becoming a Priest

Entry into Boccob's priesthood requires demonstrated arcane aptitude. The church accepts both wizard-clerics (unusual but not rare in this faith) and conventional clerics with a scholarly orientation. Candidates are evaluated on their existing knowledge base, their intellectual rigor, and their commitment to neutrality. A candidate who says they want to join because they want to use the church's resources to fight evil is politely declined. A candidate who says they want to join because they want access to the church's collection of pre-Suloise texts and they're willing to contribute to the church's knowledge base in exchange is more likely to succeed.

Training is structured as scholarship — the candidate studies under a senior cleric, produces research of some kind, and is evaluated on the quality of that research as their ordination examination.

### Ranks & Hierarchy

- **Apprentice of the Eye:** Trainee, under study.
- **Keeper of the Pentagram:** Full initiate. Working scholar-priest.
- **Archivist:** Mid-rank. Manages a specific section of the church's collection or oversees a specific research program.
- **Loremaster:** Senior clergy. Commands significant ecclesiastical authority and has typically produced substantial original research.
- **High Loremaster:** Regional leader. One per major geographical zone.
- **The Grand Archivist:** Nominal supreme authority of the church. In practice, a position held by the most respected scholar-priest in the Flanaess at any given time, determined by peer recognition rather than appointment or election. The current Grand Archivist maintains no fixed residence and is believed to be traveling continuously between the church's major archives.

### Duties & Obligations

Clergy are expected to: contribute original research to the church's archives annually, maintain a current personal spellbook or research journal (for those with arcane capacity), provide consultation services to the community (magical identification, divination services, scholarly advice), and preserve any magical texts or artifacts they encounter rather than destroying or selling them.

Clergy are explicitly not expected to: fight on behalf of any alignment or faction, proselytize aggressively, or recruit members who lack genuine scholarly interest.

### Vestments & Appearance

The church has no mandatory uniform for daily life — individual scholar-priests dress as their preferences and resources allow. Ceremonial vestments are purple and gold: deep purple robes with gold thread in geometric patterns. The eye-within-a-pentagram symbol is embroidered at the chest. The aesthetic is explicitly academic rather than martial — there are no plate armor options in the vestments catalogue, and this is considered a feature.

## ⚔️ Holy Orders & Sects

### The Arcane Archive
The largest single institution associated with Boccob's church — not an order in a military sense but an institution of scholar-priests whose entire purpose is the collection, preservation, and cataloguing of magical knowledge. Arcane Archive chapters exist in every major city in the Flanaess. Each one is simultaneously a library, a magical consultation service, and a temple. The Archive is the church's most visible public face and the reason most people have a positive association with Boccob's faith even if they know little about its theology.

### The Eyes of Tomorrow
The church's divination specialists — a formal order of priests and wizards dedicated to the development and practice of foresight magic. The Eyes of Tomorrow maintain a network of scrying assets and practice a form of institutional divination: regular group sessions in which multiple practitioners coordinate their divination attempts to produce more accurate readings of distant events. Their readings are not public, but their services can be commissioned. Governments, merchant consortia, and military commanders pay significant amounts for Eyes of Tomorrow assessments of specific questions.

### The Neutral Ground Fellowship
A semi-formal network of Boccob's clergy who specialize in mediation and neutral facilitation — providing venues and facilitators for meetings between factions that cannot trust each other. A negotiation conducted on Boccob's neutral ground is considered (by convention across multiple factions) to be genuinely safe — the church's neutrality extends to guaranteeing that neither party will be harmed during proceedings held under its auspices. This guarantee has been violated twice in recorded history; both times, the church's response was extensive and did not leave ambiguity about whether the convention would be upheld going forward.

### The Reclaimers
A field-active order within the church whose purpose is recovering magical knowledge that has been lost, hidden, or destroyed. Reclaimers are adventurers who operate with the church's formal sanction and support — they explore ruins, recover spell scrolls and magical texts from dangerous locations, negotiate (or compete) for access to private collections, and investigate reports of magical scholarship in remote regions. They are the church's most physically capable members and have a reputation as extraordinarily capable dungeon-delvers with excellent magical resources.

## 🙏 Worshippers

### Primary Worshippers

- **Wizards and sorcerers** who want institutional support, access to research resources, and the cultural legitimacy that comes with church association.
- **Scholars and sages** whose research touches on magical topics — even non-practitioners may find the church's libraries invaluable.
- **Magic-item crafters** who benefit from the church's material supply networks and its recognition of their craft as a form of scholarship.
- **Magical academies and universities** that align themselves with Boccob as their patron institution.

### Secondary Worshippers

- Merchants who deal in magical goods and want the ethical cover of church association.
- Rulers who want access to the church's divination services and are willing to formally associate with the faith.
- Adventurers who have discovered that having a good relationship with the church dramatically simplifies access to magical identification, research assistance, and knowledge of what they've found.

### Who Should NOT Approach This Faith

- Those who want the church to fight for their cause. The church does not fight causes; it preserves knowledge.
- Those who would destroy magical knowledge for ideological reasons. The church considers this the nearest thing to a sin its theology contains.
- Worshippers of Tharizdun. The church has a policy here: any Tharizdunian encountered in one of its institutions is to be reported immediately to the regional High Loremaster. The consumption of magic and magical knowledge is the church's one absolute enemy.

## 🕯️ Prayers, Rituals & Holy Days

### Daily Practice

Clergy of Boccob begin each day with the Study Hour — a structured period of arcane research, spellbook review, or meditation on magical theory. The Study Hour is considered the church's primary daily observance. Evening practice is simpler: recording the day's intellectual discoveries or failures in a personal journal that doubles as a religious document.

### Holy Days & Festivals

**The Observance of Stars (winter solstice):** The church's major annual holy day. Clergy spend the entire longest night of the year in uninterrupted arcane study — the goal is to complete a piece of research or scholarship that they have been working toward during the year. Some clergy make their most significant discoveries during the Observance, which the church attributes to the concentrated intention of the night rather than to luck.

**The Festival of Foresight (spring):** A public-facing event in which the church opens its divination archives for a day and provides free or heavily discounted divination consultations to anyone who asks. The Festival is the church's most significant outreach event and has the practical effect of generating enormous goodwill among populations who would not otherwise engage with it.

**Non-observance of weekly holidays:** The church explicitly does not observe weekly or monthly holy days. The theology holds that regular ritual interruption of scholarly work is counterproductive, and that Boccob is honored more by continued research than by regular interruption of it.

### Rites of Passage

- **The Opening of the Eye:** Ordination. The new Keeper of the Pentagram presents their research to a committee of Lorekeepers, who evaluate it and accept or reject them into full standing. If accepted, they are given a personal copy of the eye-within-pentagram symbol, cast in silver, to wear.
- **The Final Study:** The funeral rite. The deceased's research materials — personal spellbook, research journals, magical experiments — are catalogued and added to the church's archive. The body is given appropriate burial by local custom. The service consists primarily of a reading of the deceased's most significant scholarly contributions.

## 🏛️ Sacred Sites & Major Temples

### The Great Archive — City of Greyhawk
The largest single repository of magical knowledge in the Flanaess, located in Greyhawk City's Artisans Quarter. The Great Archive is simultaneously a temple, a library, a magical research institution, and the closest thing the Free City has to a neutral information clearinghouse. It holds over forty thousand volumes, several thousand spell scrolls of varying power, and a collection of magical artifacts that are too dangerous to use but too valuable to destroy. The Great Archive is open to qualified researchers — the qualification process involves demonstrating genuine scholarly interest and the ability to pay the archive fees. Clergy of Boccob from anywhere in the Flanaess receive free access.

### The Spire of Foresight — Greyhawk City
A narrower institution adjacent to the Great Archive, staffed exclusively by Eyes of Tomorrow. The Spire conducts the church's most sensitive divination work and provides commissioned readings for those who can afford them. The Spire's current assessments of major Flanaess events are known to rulers as far away as Furyondy and the Baklunish West.

### The Archive at Rel Mord — Nyrond
The second-largest Boccob archive, maintained by the church under an agreement with Nyrond's crown. Nyrond provides security and building maintenance; the church provides consultation services to the crown's government. The arrangement has been in place for two centuries and is generally considered beneficial to both parties.

### The Wandering Library
A Reclaimer institution that moves — a large covered wagon, heavily warded and magically expanded inside, that contains a rotating collection of recovered texts. The Wandering Library travels routes across the Flanaess that take it through regions where the church's fixed archives don't reach, making its collection available temporarily and returning acquired texts to the nearest fixed archive. The Reclaimers who staff it are experienced travelers and are known by communities along their routes.

### The Isle of the Mage — Lendore Isles
A private institution associated with Boccob's faith rather than formally under it — a small island that serves as a retreat for magical scholars who need uninterrupted working time. Access requires either church membership at the Archivist level or above, or an invitation from a current resident. The Isle has no formal facilities — visitors bring their own provisions — but it has extraordinary magical quiet and is considered the best place in the Flanaess to conduct extended magical research without distraction.

## ⚖️ Divine Relations

### Allies

**Celestian:** The god of stars and wandering shares Boccob's commitment to knowledge and his relative indifference to alignment conflicts. The two faiths cooperate naturally — astronomical observation has deep connections to divination and magical calendar-keeping.

**Wee Jas:** Magic and death overlap significantly in necromancy and in the scholarly tradition. Wee Jas and Boccob are not warm allies, but their churches cooperate frequently on questions of magical scholarship, and Wee Jas's emphasis on magical law and regulation aligns well with Boccob's institutional approach.

**Rao:** Peace is useful for scholarship. The god of peace's commitment to reason and intellectual discourse resonates with Boccob's theological emphasis on knowledge. The two faiths maintain respectful relations.

### Rivals & Neutral Relations

**Istus:** Fate and foresight overlap considerably. Istus's weavers and Boccob's Eyes of Tomorrow have an ongoing, polite professional competition over whose divinations are more accurate. The faiths are not hostile but are not fully trusting either.

**Most divine powers, generally:** Boccob's church does not take sides. Most other faiths find this either admirable or infuriating depending on when they need assistance.

### Enemies

**Tharizdun:** The entity of entropy and consumption is the church's singular institutional enemy. Tharizdun's portfolio includes the potential unmaking of the fabric of reality — and magic is that fabric. The church maintains active intelligence operations against Tharizdun's cults and is one of the few institutions in the Flanaess that cooperates across alignment lines on Tharizdun-specific threats.

**Iuz:** The cambion's use of magic for personal tyranny and his destruction of magical institutions in conquered territories makes him an enemy of the church in practice, even if the church's theology does not officially declare enemies.

## 🎲 Player's Guide to This Faith

### Seeking Help From This Church

Boccob's church is one of the most reliably useful churches to have a relationship with as an adventurer. It will identify magical items (at cost, and the cost is reasonable — they want to know what you found as much as you want to know what it is). It will provide access to its library for research. It will consult on magical problems. It will broker access to Eyes of Tomorrow divination services.

What it will not do: fight your battles, endorse your cause, or provide resources for free if you're not a member. The church is transactional, not charitable. Its currency is knowledge — bring interesting things you've found, bring manuscripts from ruins, bring information about magical phenomena you've encountered, and the church becomes much more generous.

### What the Church Wants From Adventurers

- **Recovery of magical texts and artifacts** — anything from a dungeon that has arcane script, a spell scroll, a magic item, or evidence of magical research. The church pays well for genuine finds and asks only for the right to copy any texts before returning the originals.
- **Access to ruined magical sites** — if the party clears a ruin that happens to have been a wizard's tower, the church wants to know and would very much like to send a Reclaimer team.
- **Information about magical phenomena** — strange magical effects in a hex, magical creatures with unusual properties, signs of magical catastrophe. The church keeps detailed records and pays for accurate reports.
- **Protection for Reclaimer teams** entering dangerous territory.

### How to Earn Favor

- Bring the church things it doesn't have. The best thing any adventuring party can give is a magical text, an unusual magical artifact, or a detailed report of a magically significant site.
- Be honest about what you've found and what you've done with it. The church can tell when you've described something inaccurately.
- Follow through on agreements. The church keeps better records than most institutions in the Flanaess.

### How to Lose Favor (Red Flags)

- Destroying magical texts or artifacts, even ones that seem dangerous. Contact the church first — they have protocols for dangerous items.
- Trying to sell the same item to the church and to another buyer simultaneously. They have ways of finding out.
- Asking the church to take sides in a conflict. They will decline, and if you push, they will remember that you pushed.

### Clerical Advice (If Playing a Priest of This Deity)

The Boccob priest is an unusual character archetype: a divine spellcaster who is technically neutral and operates outside the usual good-versus-evil frame. The theological commitment to neutrality is not cynicism — it's a genuine belief that the preservation of arcane knowledge matters more than any particular faction's victory. This creates interesting tension in a party that usually has a clear moral alignment.

The church's institutional access is a significant roleplaying resource: anywhere there is a Boccob archive, your character has a home, contacts, and resources. Use the church's network actively. And remember that "The Uncaring" is not an insult to your character — your character cares deeply, just not about the same things everyone else cares about.
