# Faith of the Common Folk

The Flanaess has dozens of recognized deities. The Sepia Uplands has perhaps four that most people actually care about. This is not ignorance — it is the natural result of living on a frontier where what you need from a god is mostly practical: keep the crops alive, hold the dark at bay, and make sure the sick child survives the winter.

What follows is not theology. It is what people here actually believe, pray for, and do when the situation requires invoking something larger than themselves.

---

## The Three Powers of Daily Life

### Pelor — *The Sun Father*
**Domains:** Light, Life, Healing, Strength
**Symbol:** A golden sun disc
**Who prays to him:** Everyone, in moments of desperation

Pelor is the most widely venerated deity in the northwestern Flanaess, and the reason is straightforward: on a frontier where winter lasts five months and the Quag Mires generate undead, the Sun Father is the most legible promise available. His light drives back undead. His healing keeps the sick from dying. His warmth is the difference between a good harvest and a bad one.

You don't have to be a cleric or a devotee to call on Pelor. Common folk light a candle to him when someone is sick, say a brief prayer at dawn when the weather looks uncertain, and credit him specifically when a child survives a fever. He is not a demanding god — he asks for gratitude expressed through looking after your neighbors, which happens to be what frontier survival requires anyway.

**In Ritterstadt:** Pelor's symbol appears above doors and on threshold stones throughout the hamlet. Vater Sigmund's chapel is dedicated to Saint Erika, but the saint is venerated as a guardian of Pelor's light in darkness — so the distinction is largely theological and most villagers don't parse it.

---

### Beory — *The Oerth Mother*
**Domains:** Nature, Earth, Cycles, Fertility
**Symbol:** A green disc
**Who prays to her:** Farmers, druids, anyone who works the land

Beory is not worshipped in the formal sense so much as acknowledged and propitiated. She is the earth itself: not a distant divine figure but the literal ground underfoot, the water in the soil, the cycle that takes the dead and makes them useful to the living. The druids at Ehlonna's Mount serve her alongside Ehlonna and consider the Sepia Uplands to be, in the deepest sense, her body.

Common folk leave small offerings at field margins during Growfest and ask nothing specific — just that the cycle continue, that the soil not turn against them, that the season be ordinary. In a Blackfrost Bloom year, Beory's silence (or preoccupation, or distress — interpretations vary) is felt as a concrete fact. The earth is not behaving normally. Something is wrong with it.

**In Ritterstadt:** No formal temple. The east forest shrine on the tree-line is a Beory-adjacent site — originally a Flan genius loci, but prayed at by most farming families regardless of their theological commitments. Leaving an offering there before planting season is considered prudent by everyone who has lived in the region long enough.

### St. Cuthbert — *The Saint of Common Sense*
**Domains:** Law, Protection, Community, Truth
**Symbol:** A crook and mace, sometimes just a wooden cudgel
**Who prays to him:** Working people, soldiers, anyone who distrusts ornamentation

St. Cuthbert is the patron of people who believe that right conduct is sufficient theology. He does not reward cleverness, beauty, or wealth — he rewards honesty, hard work, and keeping your word. His clerics carry cudgels and speak bluntly. His faith has no use for elaborate ritual, which makes it well-suited to frontier settlements where no one has time for elaborate ritual.

On the northern frontier, devotion to St. Cuthbert often expresses itself as a general expectation that people should do what they said they would, that cheats should face proportionate consequences, and that the community should function first and philosophical questions be asked later.

**In Ritterstadt:** Hauptmann Hildegard Stahlwulf — the garrison commander — follows St. Cuthbert. This is considered appropriate. Several of the farming families maintain small household shrines to him, especially the ones who have been cheated in trade in the past and have opinions about it.

## The Patron of Ritterstadt

### Saint Erika of the Long Night
**A localized aspect of [[library/deities/Pholtus]], The Blinding Light**
**Symbol:** A single torch with a halo on a dark field
**Who prays to her:** Travelers, those who keep watch, people enduring something long and dark

Saint Erika is not a Flanaess-wide figure — she is specific to the uplands frontier. The story goes that she was a woman (some say a cleric of Pholtus, some say simply a woman of faith) who kept a lantern burning in a mountain pass through an entire three-month winter so that travelers would not lose their way. Whether she was real in the historical sense is beside the point. What she represents — the kept vigil, the maintained light, the refusal to let the dark win through sheer endurance — is exactly what Ritterstadt requires of its residents.

Pholtus as a broader deity is associated with absolute law and a degree of zealotry that can make him uncomfortable to deal with in his more doctrinaire forms. Saint Erika is a softer invocation: not Pholtus's certainty, but his light, filtered through a specifically human act of stubborn persistence.

**In Ritterstadt:** St. Erika's Chapel is the hamlet's only formal place of worship, maintained by [[people/townsfolk/Vater Sigmund Wolfherz]]. Vigil candles are kept burning at all times. The midnight bell that marks the Watch Against the Dark — when someone walks the palisade with a lantern — is a Saint Erika tradition observed every night without exception, even when nothing threatens.

## The Old Faith

**Ehlonna, Beory, and the Living World**

Before Oeridian settlement brought its organized religions to the northwestern Flanaess, the Flan practiced a form of the Old Faith: a relationship with the living land, its spirits, and the divine forces that animate the natural world. This is not a dead tradition. The druid enclave at Ehlonna's Mount is forty miles southeast and actively maintains the standing stone network, the bog surveys, and the most accurate ecological records in the region.

The Old Faith does not proselytize, does not build temples, and does not require formal conversion. What it does require is respect for the living world as something with genuine spiritual weight — not background scenery for human ambition. Students who treat the uplands as simply "terrain" will find the druids uninterested in helping them. Students who demonstrate a more nuanced appreciation may find the enclave's extensive knowledge of the region's natural and supernatural cycles to be an extraordinary resource.

**Genius loci** — local land spirits — are real in the Sepia Uplands. They are not powerful enough to threaten an adventuring party in conventional terms. They are powerful enough to make an area hostile, to spoil stored food, to agitate animals, and to send dreams outward to those nearby. The east forest shrine at the edge of Ritterstadt's tree-line has one. It has been unsettled lately.

## What to Do When Religion Comes Up at the Table

You don't have to play a religious character. Almost no one in the Sepia Uplands is formally devout — they are pragmatically faithful, which is different. What matters is knowing which powers are locally relevant, understanding that the common folk take these things seriously even when they're not performing formal worship, and not casually desecrating shrines unless you've thought about the downstream consequences.

If your character follows a deity not listed here, they exist in the world — the Flanaess has a broad pantheon — but they will be unusual in this specific setting. That can be interesting. It will require you to explain yourself occasionally.
