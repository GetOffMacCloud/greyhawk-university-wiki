# RQ-002 — On a Juvenile Xorn Encounter at the Flan Waymark Interior (Hex E3), with Post-Fact Observations on a Material Plane Family Unit

**Greyhawk University · Ritterstadt Satellite Station · Field Survey Report**

**Authors (alphabetical by surname, all enrolled Freshman Cohort 576 CY):** Bethany McCockin-Simmons, Bromm StoneWarden, Flora Gibson, Felix Fleetfoot, Tuldrim Hammerfall, Rowena Ithilbrook

**Faculty Reviewer:** Sigmund Holt, Field Research Officer (Ritterstadt Satellite Station)

**Submitted:** 12 Flocktime, 576 CY
**Manuscript Reference:** RQ-002 / Sepia Uplands Cryptozoological Series, Vol. I
**Specimen Reference:** SP-576-022 (xorn hide, full, intact, deposited Ritterstadt Specimen Archive)

---

## Abstract

We report the observation, engagement, and harvest of a single juvenile xorn (order *Geomida*, family *Xorniformes*) encountered within the interior chamber complex of the Flan Waymark at Hex E3 during the concluding session of Operation GHU1 on the date of 12 Planting 576 CY. The specimen presented at approximately three-fifths the volumetric mass of the standard adult xorn morphology recorded in the existing literature, with proportionally reduced dentition and an attack repertoire restricted to manual rather than mandibular engagement. Combat with the specimen lasted approximately eight rounds and concluded with the specimen's death. Subsequent reconnaissance of the adjoining octagonal sanctum revealed the deceased adult of the species and a nest containing one broken and one absent egg. The implications of this find — that xorn maintain breeding family units on the Material Plane and that the encountered juvenile was acting in defence of its surviving family circle — were not legible to the witnesses until after the engagement had concluded, and form the central scholarly contribution and the central regret of this report.

---

## 1. Introduction

The xorn is well-attested in the surface literature as a creature of the Plane of Earth, occasionally encountered on the Material Plane as a transient via earth-glide or planar slippage. The standard literature treats the species as a solitary scavenger drawn to subsurface concentrations of precious metals and gemstones, with no documented family or breeding behaviour and no standing recognition of subadult morphological variants.

The present observation diverges from the standing literature in three respects of academic significance.

First, the encountered specimen was morphologically and developmentally a *subadult* — distinct from the adult by mass, dentition, and behavioural register — confirming that xorn possess a juvenile life stage and that the species does, in fact, breed. The standing literature has not addressed this because no juvenile specimen has, to the witnesses' knowledge, been previously documented.

Second, the post-engagement discovery of an adjacent adult specimen (deceased, in advanced post-mortem state) accompanied by a structured nesting site containing oviparous remains establishes that xorn breeding occurs on the Material Plane and that family units do exist. The standing assumption that xorn presence on the Material Plane is transient and solitary is, on the strength of this single observation, falsified.

Third, the juvenile's behavioural pattern during engagement — sustained territorial defence, focused targeting on the single party member visibly carrying mineralised stone (a chip of secondary-compound ferrotholite affixed to a chain-mail strap), and pattern of disengage-and-re-emerge via earth glide consistent with terrain familiarity rather than blind reaction — is consistent with *home defence* rather than opportunistic predation. The juvenile was not hunting the party. The juvenile was responding to a threat to its remaining family.

The witnesses record these findings with the academic honesty the situation requires.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Engagement Context

The party entered the interior chamber complex of the Flan Waymark at approximately mid-morning on 12 Planting following the resolution of the secondary-vein ore extraction phase of the principal commission. The juvenile specimen presented in the Relay Alcove (interior Room 4) upon the party's traverse of the chamber's central axis. The encounter was unanticipated; the witnesses had not been briefed to expect elemental fauna at this site.

The party also engaged, concurrently, a single mimic (*Polymorphia composita*) presenting as a small bound chest within the same chamber. Combat thus proceeded on two fronts simultaneously, with the party dividing as follows:

| Mimic engagement (south wall) | Xorn engagement (chamber centre) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Fleetfoot (Rogue), Gibson (Druid, brown-bear form), McCockin-Simmons (Paladin) | StoneWarden (Paladin), Hammerfall (Fighter), Ithilbrook (Cleric) |

The split engagement is noted because it materially affected the duration and tactical character of the xorn encounter. We submit, candidly, that the mimic's intervention absorbed a substantial fraction of the party's available actions and may have been the difference between a survivable encounter and a casualty event.

### 2.2 Observational Limitations

The encounter was a live combat. Field measurement was not feasible during the engagement; the specimen's dimensions, weight, and morphological detail were recorded post-mortem with surveyor's cord and field scale. Behavioural observations during combat were recorded by recollection within four hours of engagement conclusion and have been cross-checked across the six witnesses.

The post-mortem examination of the adult specimen in the adjoining sanctum chamber (Room 5) was conducted with appropriate care for what it was. The adult had been dead for an estimated three to seven days based on tissue condition. The nest and its contents were not disturbed beyond what was necessary to confirm what they were. No specimen was taken from the adult or from the nest.

### 2.3 Witness Composition

All six members of the Ritterstadt Questers were present for the juvenile engagement. All six were present for the post-fact discovery of the adult and the nest. Affidavit content is in Appendix A.

## 3. Field Observations — The Juvenile Specimen

### 3.1 Morphology

The specimen, post-mortem, measured as follows:

| Measurement | Value | Adult Comparison (literature standard) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Height (apex of crown to base) | 0.98 m | ~1.5 m |
| Maximum girth (mid-body) | 1.42 m circumference | ~2.0 m |
| Mass (estimated post-harvest) | ~145 kg | ~250 kg |
| Manual digit count | 3 (consistent with adult morphology) | 3 |
| Manual claw length | 8.2 cm average | ~12 cm |
| Crown-ridge sensory papillae | Present, fully developed | Present |
| Mandibular dentition | **Absent or undeveloped** | Present, multi-row |
| Integument | Stone-bound, dark grey, lithoidal | Stone-bound, dark grey, lithoidal |
| Estimated hit-capacity | ~50 hit points (estimated by damage absorbed) | ~73 hit points (literature standard) |

The morphological consistency with the adult form is total in all respects *except* the mandibular dentition and overall mass. The juvenile was, in form and proportion, recognisably the same species; it was simply *smaller*, with a developmentally incomplete jaw structure.

This finding is the paper's primary morphological contribution. The standing literature does not record an undeveloped-dentition juvenile xorn morphology. We submit it for the consideration of the Department of Anomalous Fauna and Aetheric Biology.

### 3.2 Combat Behaviour

The specimen demonstrated four behavioural characteristics worthy of academic note:

**Behaviour 1 — Tremorsense Targeting.** The specimen oriented within the first round of engagement on the single party member (StoneWarden) carrying a chip of secondary-compound ferrotholite affixed to a chain-mail strap. The orientation was clearly tremor-based: the specimen never visually tracked StoneWarden, and yet emerged in a position to engage him in 6 of 7 surfacing instances. The ferrotholite chip — approximately 4 cm³ in volume — produced a tremorsense signature substantially distinguishable from the surrounding metallic loads of the rest of the party despite being many orders of magnitude smaller than any single piece of metal armor in the room.

This finding has tactical implications for any future expedition operating in the Sepia Uplands. **The carriage of ferrotholite specimens on one's person is contraindicated when xorn are a possibility.** Future field protocol will house secondary-vein specimens in shielded ceramic carriers and not on the worker's body.

**Behaviour 2 — Earth-Glide Tactical Use.** The specimen used Earth Glide tactically rather than as a flight response. It disengaged from melee on three separate occasions and re-surfaced in the rear flank of whoever had most recently struck it. This is consistent with deliberate combat repositioning, not panic. The specimen treated the chamber's stone floor as a third dimension. This is well-attested behaviour in the adult; we note its presence in the juvenile as a developmental matter (the capacity is present at the juvenile stage and is not learned post-maturity).

**Behaviour 3 — Restricted Attack Repertoire.** The specimen attacked with two manual claw strikes per engagement round and made no bite attempts. This is consistent with the undeveloped mandibular dentition recorded post-mortem. The juvenile's reduced threat profile is a real morphological reality, not a behavioural choice.

**Behaviour 4 — Persistence.** The specimen did not flee at any point during the engagement, including when reduced to substantially below 50% of estimated hit-capacity. Adult xorn in the literature are described as opportunistic and willing to disengage when overmatched. The juvenile here did not disengage. **In hindsight, the reason is the contents of Room 5.** The juvenile was not fighting for opportunity. It was holding ground.

## 4. Post-Fact Discovery — Room 5 (The Adjoining Octagonal Sanctum)

Following the juvenile's death and the dispatch of the concurrent mimic, the party proceeded to the adjacent chamber (Room 5, the Octagonal Sanctum) to recover the primary academic objective of the expedition.

In the southeast quadrant of the sanctum, against the wall, lay the body of an adult xorn. Estimated time-of-death three to seven days prior to discovery. Cause of death not externally apparent — no obvious wound, no defensive posture; the body was curled in a manner suggesting natural decline rather than combat. The proximate cause is, on the witnesses' best assessment, age or environmental factor; the chamber's resonance bleed (well-documented in the Waymark's interior environment) may have been a contributing or principal cause.

Beside the adult, a low nest of stone-shavings, bone fragments, and softened mineral matter, of dimensions consistent with the literature's description of xorn nesting on the Plane of Earth (a body of literature so sparse it amounts to two references and a marginal note). In the nest:

- **One broken egg.** Approximately 18 cm at long axis, leathery shell of a translucent grey, ruptured along a single radial seam. The interior was empty and cold; no fluid, no contents, no embryo. The egg had not hatched. The contents had not survived to be hatched.
- **One indentation in the nest matrix corresponding to a second egg, absent.** The indentation was the same size and shape as the broken-egg envelope. The second egg had been present and had been removed — *not* hatched, on the evidence of the absence of any shell fragment in the nest or chamber.

The conclusion is unavoidable. The juvenile xorn the party engaged in Room 4 was the surviving sibling of the unhatched broken-egg embryo and was the offspring of the deceased adult in Room 5. The family unit consisted of:

- One adult (deceased ~3–7 days prior to encounter, cause of death uncertain, body in situ)
- One juvenile (the engaged specimen, now deceased)
- One unhatched broken-egg embryo (the sibling, never hatched)
- One absent egg-indentation (provenance unknown — removed, taken, or never present in the form documented)

The juvenile's persistent territorial defence in Room 4 is, on this evidence, *home defence.* It was holding the corridor between the party's approach and the sanctum where its parent's body and its sibling's nest lay.

The witnesses ask for nothing in the academic record on this point beyond the bare factual observation. The party did not know what was in Room 5 when they engaged the juvenile in Room 4. They engaged in good faith on the information they had. The post-fact reframing of the encounter does not undo the encounter. It does, however, change what subsequent expeditions ought to do.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Implications for the Xorn Literature

If the present finding generalises — and the witnesses are aware that a single observation does not establish a generalisation — then the standing characterisation of xorn as solitary Planar transients is incorrect or incomplete. Xorn maintain family units. Xorn breed via oviparous reproduction on at least the Material Plane and likely elsewhere. Xorn possess a juvenile life stage with developmental traits (reduced mass, incomplete dentition) that have not previously been recorded.

The witnesses note that the standing literature is sparse on this species — perhaps a dozen reliable observations in the last two centuries. A single contradicting observation is therefore disproportionately weighty.

### 5.2 Why Here?

The presence of an established xorn family unit in the interior chamber complex of a 4,000-year-old Flan Waymark is improbable. The Waymark is not an obvious xorn habitat (it is constructed stone, not natural earth; the surrounding escarpment, while suitable, is not exceptional). The witnesses' working hypothesis, advanced tentatively and pending confirmation, is that the family was drawn to the same resonance bleed phenomenon that draws other anomalous fauna to the Sepia Uplands — see [[RQ-0001 — On the Aerial Persistence of an Anomalous Selachian Swarm Observed Above the Ritterstadt–Ochre Escarpment Road|RQ-0001 §5.2]] on this point. The Waymark's structural stone carries a continuous low-frequency resonance signature. The mineral byproducts of that resonance — concentrated ferrotholite traces in the immediate substrate — may be detectable by xorn tremorsense at substantial distance. The family may have come here, in plain language, because the food was good.

If true, this implies that as the binding network's degradation continues, xorn presence in the Sepia Uplands may *increase* rather than decrease. Future expeditions should anticipate the possibility.

### 5.3 Recommendations for Subsequent Expeditions

- **Carry ferrotholite specimens in shielded ceramic containers.** Do not affix mineralised stone to one's person under any circumstances.
- **Attempt parley before engagement.** Xorn speak Terran. They are intelligent (literature INT 11, observation consistent). The standing tribute path (50 gp of precious metal or gem in exchange for non-engagement) is, on the witnesses' present view, the *preferred* approach. The juvenile here was not approached for parley; the engagement opened with combat. We cannot say whether parley would have succeeded. We can say it was not attempted.
- **Map family contexts before engaging.** If a xorn is encountered in a confined site, a brief reconnaissance of the surrounding chambers may reveal a nest or family member. The encounter changes character entirely if a family unit is present.
- **Preserve nest sites.** If a xorn family is encountered and engagement occurs, the witnesses recommend that any surviving nest material be preserved in situ rather than disturbed. The academic value of an undisturbed xorn nest, post the present finding, is substantial.

### 5.4 The Hide (SP-576-022)

The hide of the juvenile specimen was harvested with care and is deposited in the Ritterstadt Specimen Archive as accession SP-576-022. The hide is intact, complete from crown to ventral midline, and substantially less weathered than would be expected of an adult specimen (consistent with the subadult morphology). It is offered to the Department of Anomalous Fauna for analytical work and to the Department of Material Studies for any lithoidal-integument research that may benefit from a subadult comparative.

The hide is offered with the understanding established in §4. Subsequent researchers should know what it is.

---

## 6. Conclusion

We document a juvenile xorn engagement at the Flan Waymark interior, providing the first recorded morphological description of a subadult xorn and the first recorded observation of a xorn family unit on the Material Plane. We propose that the standing literature treating xorn as solitary Planar transients be amended to reflect the possibility of resident, breeding family groups in Material Plane locations of geological or magical significance. We submit the hide of the engaged specimen for academic analysis and the field observations of the deceased adult and the nest for the consideration of the faculty.

The encounter was completed before the family unit was understood. The witnesses cannot retroactively change that. They can only place on the record, in the appropriate academic register, that subsequent expeditions need not make the same observation in the same order.

## Appendix A — Witness Affidavits

The following statements were composed independently within four hours of the engagement and the discovery of the family context, and have been transcribed without editorial alteration.

> **Bethany McCockin-Simmons** *(Paladin, Oath of Trithereon):* I made this account with the same hand that landed strikes against the specimen. I record it for the academic file because the academic file is the right place for it. Trithereon's tenets concern liberty; the creature was defending what it had left. I will be thinking about that for a long time.

> **Bromm StoneWarden** *(Hill Dwarf Paladin, Oath of Vengeance):* The ore chip in my armor strap was a foolish carrying decision. The creature came for me because of it. I do not blame the creature. I record the lesson — for myself and for whoever reads this — that what you carry on you in the field, the field will use to find you. I am sorry for what was in the next room.

> **Felix Fleetfoot** *(Lightfoot Halfling Rogue):* I opened the chest. The chest was not a chest. I confirm the account as written. I will note that I have been a thief for longer than I have been a student, and that this is the first chest I have ever opened that I have regretted opening for reasons other than its contents.

> **Rowena Ithilbrook** *(Valley Elf Cleric, Nature Domain):* I knelt by the nest. I did not pray. The Domain does not ask for that. I placed my hand near the broken egg and registered what was in the room — the residue of a small life that had been making space for a smaller life. I confirm everything written above. I would like the record to note that I would like a return expedition to seal the nest properly. I will not insist on it. I will ask.

> **Tuldrim Hammerfall** *(Mountain Dwarf Fighter, Furyondian Crusader):* I confirm the account. My giant-dodger training served against an opponent that emerged in unexpected positions, which was a use of the training I had not anticipated. I have nothing further to add to the academic record. I have things to add to the private one.

> **Flora Gibson** *(Rock Gnome Druid, Circle of the Moon):* I was a spider for the stirges. I was a bear for the mimic. I was a gnome in the sanctum. The sanctum is the form that mattered. I confirm the account. The bear has opinions about the family. They are not academic.
