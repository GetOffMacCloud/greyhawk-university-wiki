## Abstract

We report the observation, on the second evening of overland travel between the Hamlet of Ritterstadt and the Ochre Escarpment (Hex E3) on the date of 24 Planting 576 CY, of a single composite aerial entity consistent with the colloquially-attested *"Sharkenbearnado"* type. The entity comprised an estimated three to four dozen individual selachian organisms, each of approximately medium humanoid mass and dentition, suspended within and traversing the landscape by means of a self-sustaining cyclonic column of approximately four metres in diameter at its base and rising to an estimated nine to twelve metres in height. The phenomenon traversed open ground at sustained pace and produced acoustic, kinetic, and biotic effects upon the surrounding environment of a severity sufficient to render direct engagement inadvisable for a research party of our composition and level of training. The phenomenon was observed for approximately six minutes from concealment, recorded in field notes immediately upon dispersal, and is here documented in the hope that its formal cataloguing will support future Sepia Uplands survey design. We propose the binomial designation *Selachodine minor* and recommend its classification under University Tier II Anomalous Fauna pending corroborating sightings.

---

## 1. Introduction

The Sepia Uplands have long supported a folk tradition of "sky-fish" sightings, generally attributed in the academic literature to mirage, intoxication, weather inversion artefacts, or the well-documented elemental upwellings associated with the Lake Quag basin (cf. *Regional Guide — The Sepia Uplands & Lake Quag*, §IV). Such sightings have been treated, with appropriate caution, as folkloric rather than zoological in the standing University literature.

The present observation diverges from this category in three respects. First, it was witnessed by a research party of six (6) trained observers in concert, all of whom independently corroborated the central morphological and kinetic features in subsequent written affidavit (see Appendix A). Second, the phenomenon was photographed by mnemonic recollection at sufficient detail to permit anatomical sketch (see Figures 1–3, attached as separate plates), and these sketches are mutually consistent. Third, the phenomenon left physical residue at its dispersal site: thirteen (13) intact selachian teeth of a morphology not consistent with any freshwater or coastal species recorded in *Hellawes, Marine Fauna of the Western Flanaess* (4th ed., 552 CY) or its supplementary volumes, recovered and submitted to the Station's specimen archive under accession SP-576-014.

The witness party has elected to publish promptly rather than defer pending further sightings, on the reasoning that field reports of low-probability anomalous fauna are most useful to subsequent surveyors when filed at the earliest opportunity and not delayed for the purpose of authorial certitude.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Observation Conditions

The party was undertaking the overland approach to the Ochre Escarpment under the standing commission of the Ritterstadt Mining Consortium (Drunik Runevault, liaison). At approximately the eighteenth hour of the second travel day, the party halted to rest the pack mule and to permit the rear scout (Fleetfoot) to confirm the absence of pursuit. During this halt, a low-frequency atmospheric oscillation was noted by three party members independently. Within approximately ninety seconds of first detection, the entity emerged above the western ridgeline at an estimated distance of three hundred and forty metres.

The party took concealment behind a limestone outcrop and observed from cover for the entirety of the entity's transit across the field of view. No attempt was made to engage. This decision, recorded by the senior member at the time of observation (Hammerfall, fighter), is defended in §6.

### 2.2 Field Instruments

Field instruments employed during the observation comprised:
- One (1) brass-tube tri-compass on loan from the Station, used to record bearing of approach and departure;
- One (1) standard surveyor's measuring cord, used post-event to estimate dispersal-site dimensions;
- The professional senses of the witnesses, including but not limited to the heightened low-light vision of three (3) party members of non-human ancestry and the *Detect Magic* orison maintained throughout the observation by the party's cleric (Ithilbrook, Valley Elf, Nature Domain).

No portable scriptorium was carried; field notes were committed to memory and transcribed within four (4) hours of the event at the party's evening camp.

### 2.3 Limitations of Method

The witnesses acknowledge the following limitations of their observational position:
- The observation distance precluded close anatomical examination of individual specimens;
- Wind conditions during observation (south-southeast, gusting) likely affected the entity's apparent speed and bearing;
- One member of the party (Gibson, druid, Circle of the Moon) elected to assume the form of a brown bear during the latter half of the observation, which while permitting enhanced concealment from line-of-sight reduced the number of fully-articulate witnesses for the final two minutes of the encounter. Her contribution to the subsequent affidavit was recorded after she resumed bipedal form and confirmed independent recollection of all features described.

## 3. Field Observations

### 3.1 Acoustic Signature

The entity was audible at the limit of human hearing for an estimated forty seconds prior to becoming visible. The signature comprised a low-frequency rotational thrum (the cyclonic column itself) modulated by an irregular, percussive snapping (interpreted post hoc as the simultaneous jaw-closure of multiple specimens within the column). Above this composite signature, several witnesses reported a higher-pitched intermittent component that one author (Ithilbrook) compared to "a kettle close to whistling, played from the bottom of a well." The cleric's *Detect Magic* indicated faint conjuration aura at distance but no clearly identifiable school upon closer approach.

### 3.2 Visual Morphology

The entity presented as a roughly cylindrical column of darkly-coloured atmospheric matter, rotating about a vertical axis, within and through which an estimated thirty-six (36) to forty-eight (48) individual selachian organisms could be discerned. The organisms varied in length from approximately one to two metres. Their integument was characteristic of pelagic shark species: dorsal countershading, lateral lines, and pectoral and caudal fin morphology entirely consistent with the order *Selachimorpha* as catalogued in the standing zoological literature.

At no point during the observation was any ursine, lupine, or other mammalian taxon detected within or proximate to the column. The party wishes to underscore this point: the colloquial designation *"Sharkenbearnado,"* which has acquired regrettable currency in the witnesses' own subsequent informal speech, is at the present level of evidence taxonomically unjustified. No bear was observed. None of the entity's components possessed mammalian morphology. The compound term is preserved in the popular literature for institutional continuity and as a courtesy to the publican of the Fiend's Rest Public House, who first coined it on hearing the witnesses' verbal account, but it is here formally deprecated.

### 3.3 Kinetic Behaviour

The entity traversed open ground at a sustained speed of approximately twelve metres per second, hovering at a constant altitude of approximately one to two metres above the surface (i.e., the lowest specimens within the column passed at roughly head-height to a standing observer). It demonstrated direct purposive locomotion across the field of view from west-southwest to east-northeast, suggesting either active steering or a strong correlation with the prevailing surface wind.

Two (2) discrete examples of predatory interaction with the surrounding environment were observed. In the first, a juvenile sepia-jack hare (*Lepus sepiaticus*) attempting to flee across the entity's path was overtaken; the column adjusted its bearing by approximately fifteen degrees to intercept, and the specimen was no longer visible following its disappearance into the rotating mass. In the second, a stand of upland thornbrush approximately two metres in height was traversed and reduced to fragments of length less than five centimetres. The witnesses observed that any organism or object passing within an apparent radius of three metres from the column's outer wall was drawn inward along a clockwise spiral consistent with the direction of the column's rotation.

### 3.4 Dispersal

After approximately six minutes of observed transit, the entity reached a low depression in the terrain where standing surface water remained from the previous day's rain. Upon contact with this water, the cyclonic column collapsed within approximately four seconds. The constituent selachian specimens fell to the ground, where they were observed to thrash for a period of between fifteen and forty seconds before becoming still. The witnesses approached the dispersal site after a precautionary wait of one quarter-hour. All specimens were dead. Cause of death is assessed by the senior cleric of the party as consistent with rapid asphyxiation in atmosphere.

This finding is, the authors submit, the central scholarly puzzle presented by the encounter and is treated at length in §5.

## 4. Proposed Taxonomic Designation

We respectfully propose the binomial designation **_Selachodine minor_** (from κοιν. Greek σέλαχος, "shark," and δίνη, "vortex" or "whirlpool"; *minor* in recognition that the observed specimen's dimensions are consistent with the smaller end of the colloquially-described range, and that more substantial congregations may yet be documented).

We further propose the following classificatory placement, contingent on faculty review:

| Field | Proposed Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Kingdom | Animalia |
| Order | Anomalous Composite (*Phenomena cohabita*) |
| Class | Aerial Selachian (*Selachimorpha aerica*) |
| Designation | *Selachodine minor* |
| Threat Tier | University Tier II (avoid engagement under Cohort Year I–II) |
| Encounter Probability (Sepia Uplands, summer) | Rare — single attested instance |
| Conservation Status | Unknown; insufficient population data |

The witnesses note that the term "swarm" is used in the abstract and conclusions for descriptive convenience but may misrepresent the entity's nature: it is unclear whether the constituent specimens are individuated organisms acting in coordinated congregation, or whether the entity should properly be regarded as a single composite organism that expresses itself through multiple subordinate masses. Resolution of this question would require either capture of a live specimen (not, at present, recommended) or comparative observation of a second instance.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 The Atmospheric Persistence Paradox

The defining scholarly difficulty presented by the encounter is the apparent contradiction between two reliably-observed facts:

1. The specimens, upon contact with the open atmosphere following the column's dispersal, expired within forty seconds in a manner consistent with the asphyxiation of obligate aquatic respirators.
2. The specimens, prior to dispersal, traversed at least three hundred metres of open atmosphere over a period of not less than six minutes without distress.

The witnesses considered and discounted the following potential resolutions:

- *Hibernation or aestivation:* Inconsistent with the observed kinetic activity of the specimens during transit, which included multiple predatory behaviours requiring sustained respiration.
- *Cutaneous respiration:* While certain elasmobranch species possess limited cutaneous gas-exchange capacity, the volume required to sustain the observed activity exceeds any documented capability of the order.
- *Magical sustenance:* The *Detect Magic* aura observed during the encounter was insufficient in intensity to account for the sustained metabolic load of forty-plus large specimens. Some magical contribution cannot be excluded, but it cannot be the whole answer.

The witnesses' present working hypothesis is that the cyclonic column itself maintains, by means presently unknown, a sustained atmosphere of aerosolised water and humidified vapour sufficient to permit the constituent specimens to respire as they would in a marine environment. The collapse of the column upon water contact would, on this hypothesis, mark the failure of the maintained micro-environment as the column's structural integrity was disrupted by the abrupt change in surface friction and humidity gradient. The expiry of the specimens in still atmosphere following dispersal would then be consistent not with the entity's *inability* to respire dry air at any point, but with the *withdrawal* of a sustained environmental adaptation upon which it relied. This is a hypothesis only. The witnesses lack the analytical apparatus to test it.

### 5.2 Possible Causal Correlates

The encounter occurred within twelve (12) miles of the Ochre Escarpment site and within approximately eight (8) miles of the Flan Waymark structure under current expedition study (see E3 - The Flan Waymark). The witnesses are aware that the proximity is suggestive, and that the existing scholarship on the Sepia Uplands has begun to admit the possibility that the region may host anomalous fauna and atmospheric phenomena at elevated baseline frequency relative to surrounding terrains. The witnesses decline to advance any specific causal claim, noting that single-observation correlation does not constitute evidence of mechanism, and that responsible field practice in this regard demands restraint until further data is available.

The witnesses do note, without further elaboration, that the entity's bearing of departure (east-northeast) is consistent with the trajectory toward the Quag Mire fringe and Hex D4, and that the publication of this paper in advance of further survey is intended in part to flag this observation for any subsequent expedition operating in those quarters.

### 5.3 Recommendations for Future Surveyors

Should a subsequent party witness an entity of the type described:

- **Do not engage at standard combat distance.** The witnesses estimate, from observed kinetic effects, that any creature passing within three metres of the column's outer wall is subject to a sustained inward draw consistent with the loss of approximately half of a healthy adult human's available action capacity per round. Engagement at melee distance is not a matter of strategy but of survivability.
- **Maintain visual contact from distance.** A higher elevation observer (ridgetop, low cliff) is preferable to a same-grade observer.
- **Record bearing of approach and departure with precision.** This data, accumulated across multiple sightings, may eventually permit predictive modelling of the entity's movement patterns and origin point.
- **Recover physical specimens from any dispersal site.** Even a single intact tooth is, at present, of higher academic value than any number of subsequent affidavits.
- **Refrain from coining additional informal designations.** The witnesses speak from regretful experience.

## 6. Defence of Non-Engagement

We anticipate, as a matter of course, the question of whether a research party of six (6) enrolled students, all of demonstrated combat competency and supported by both divine and primal magical assets, ought to have attempted closer examination of the specimen by means of engagement. We submit the following considered response.

The party's composite assessment of the entity's combat capability, made in real time at observed distance, indicated:
- Hit point capacity in excess of any single party member's available damage in a full encounter round;
- Resistance or immunity to standard mundane weapon damage of the three classical types;
- An area-effect drawing field of approximately three metres radius producing both kinetic displacement and piercing damage on a sustained basis;
- An ability to engage multiple targets simultaneously via the constituent specimens' independent dentition.

The senior fighter of the party (Hammerfall), citing his discipline's emphasis on prudent assessment of force differential, recommended observation without engagement. The senior paladin (StoneWarden, Oath of Vengeance) seconded the assessment despite a noted contrary impulse. The cleric (Ithilbrook) confirmed that her healing capacity was insufficient to sustain a full party through expected damage exposure. The motion to observe rather than engage was carried unanimously, with the rogue (Fleetfoot) abstaining on the formal grounds that he had already secured a concealed position prior to the discussion. The decision, in the witnesses' considered post-hoc assessment, was correct.

We submit further that the academic value of the observations recorded here would have been substantially diminished by an engagement that the party would, in the witnesses' frank judgment, almost certainly not have survived in coherent enough form to publish.

---

## 7. Conclusion

We document the observation of a single instance of an aerial composite selachian entity of the type folklorically attested in the Sepia Uplands but not previously confirmed by trained observation. We propose the binomial designation *Selachodine minor* and recommend Tier II classification. We submit the encounter for the consideration of the faculty and for the awareness of subsequent field parties operating in the region. We commend the question of atmospheric persistence to the faculty of the Department of Anomalous Fauna and Aetheric Biology, in the hope that a more capable cohort will, in due course, document a specimen at closer quarters and resolve the matter satisfactorily.

We close by noting that the colloquial term *"Sharkenbearnado,"* notwithstanding our formal deprecation of it, has proven impossible to extirpate from the witnesses' own private speech and from the discourse of the Fiend's Rest Public House. We submit this fact in the spirit of intellectual honesty.

## Appendix A — Witness Affidavits

The following statements were composed independently within four (4) hours of the encounter and have been transcribed without editorial alteration.

> **Bethany McCockin-Simmons** *(Paladin, Oath of Trithereon):* I saw it clearly. The thing rotated counter to the wind, which I noted at the time and have not stopped thinking about since. Trithereon teaches liberation; nothing in his teaching prepares one for the liberation of sharks from the sea. I commend the entity to no deity I currently serve.

> **Bromm StoneWarden** *(Hill Dwarf Paladin, Oath of Vengeance):* I make my mark on this account as accurate. The thing was a shape against the sky no dwarf should have to see. I felt no calling from my oath. The entity has not, to my knowledge, wronged me personally.

> **Felix Fleetfoot** *(Lightfoot Halfling Rogue):* I was in cover before the others. From my position I could see the air *bend* around it. The mouse stayed in my pocket and did not look out, which I respect. I confirm everything in the document above and add only that I will be the first one to leave the road if I ever hear that low hum again.

> **Rowena Ithilbrook** *(Valley Elf Cleric, Nature Domain):* The aura was faint but present, and unlike any conjuration I have studied. The wrongness of it is difficult to convey in academic language. The land beneath the entity's path felt — and I choose this word with caution — *unenthusiastic.* I do not know what else to call it. The plants did not look up.

> **Tuldrim Hammerfall** *(Mountain Dwarf Fighter, Furyondian Crusader):* Recommended observation. Recommendation accepted. Account above is accurate to the best of my recollection. If a future party encounters this entity, I urge them to make the same recommendation.

> **Flora Gibson** *(Rock Gnome Druid, Circle of the Moon):* I was a bear for most of it. I confirm the rest. The bear form is well-suited to remaining unobtrusive in upland scrub and is recommended for any future druid encountering this entity. The bear also has opinions about sharks. They are not academic.
