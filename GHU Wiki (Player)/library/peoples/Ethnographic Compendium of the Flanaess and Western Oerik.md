# Ethnographic Compendium of the Flanaess and Western Oerik

## 1. Introduction: The Strategic Importance of Lineage

In the meticulously maintained records of the Greyhawk Online Research Initiative, racial origin is never viewed as a mere biological happenstance. Rather, archival evidence suggests it is the primary determinant of a character’s political trajectory, ethical framework, and survival capacity within the geopolitical machinery of Oerth. To select a lineage is to inherit a specific vantage point within a living historical struggle—transforming a character from a collection of statistics into a participant in the shifting tides of power.

**The Historical Participant** The "So What?" of character creation lies in how regional shifts—from the agonizing rot of the House of Rax to the revolutionary fire of the People’s Republic of Mordengard—dictate immediate survival. A character’s heritage provides access to specialized starting equipment and tactical assets: a Flan tribesman begins with the knowledge to craft Usk-leaf armor, while a Mordengard dwarf might carry the "Leadblaster" of the Udarnik. Whether navigating the aristocratic decline of the Great Kingdom or the guild-based meritocracy of Western Oerik, a character’s ancestry defines their "why" in a world where ancient artifacts like the Regalia of Might are no longer myths, but strategic objectives.

## 2. The Human Diaspora: Oerdian, Flan, and the Rhennee Tribes

Human civilization in the Flanaess is a study in fragmentation. Our records indicate a landscape split between the decadent remnants of the Great Kingdom and the resilient, often overlooked nomadic tribes of the wilderness.

### The Oerdian and Flan Heritage

- **Oerdian Demeanor:** Shaped by the "Turmoil Between Crowns" and the fall of the House of Rax, Oerdians (particularly of Houses Rax and Cranden) often grapple with aristocratic decline. Their narratives are frequently defined by the year 203 CY—the fall of Prince Myrhal—and a desperate desire to reclaim the chivalry of a lost golden age.
- **Flan and Nehron Extraction:** Characters of "Ancient Blood" archetypes often hail from Flan stock, such as the ancient Haradaragh. Furthermore, archival entries regarding the lich-lord Seragrimm suggest that some human lineages are of ancient **Nehron extraction**, providing a layer of historical depth for those seeking a connection to the Eldritch Lords of old.

### The Rhennee Distinction: Rhel-far vs. Att-loi

The Rhennee represent a non-indigenous culture whose path split during the expulsion of 141 CY.

- **The Att-loi:** These tribes sought the west, maintaining their independence.
- **The Rhel-far:** Driven by malevolence, this tribe remained in the Adri Forest. Archival records confirm they serve the Death Knight Myrhal as spies. Critically, the Rhel-far serve Myrhal because his commanding presence reminds them of the **Despots of Rhop**, the ancient tyrants of their own legends. They often pose as Att-loi to move undetected through civilized lands.

### Cultural Assets: The Usk Tree and Uski Tribes

In the Jotens, the isolationist **Uski tribesmen** have mastered the bounty of the Usk tree. For a survival-focused character, these assets are transformative:

- **Usk-leaf Armor:** Crafted from cured leaves, this provides an **AC of 8**. However, the material is fragile, lasting only **3d6 days** before requiring replacement.
- **The Siege Sling:** A signature weapon of the Uski, this sling+2 features a pocket made of Usk leaf. On a natural 20, the projectile enlarges into a boulder, striking with the force of a cloud giant’s rock (1d10+11 damage).

## 3. The Dwur (Dwarven) Kingdoms: Monarchy and Republic

Dwarven identity is anchored in the "Low Road"—the subterranean network that, according to our chronicles, allows the Dwur to place their enemies **"between the hammer and the anvil."**

### The Lortmil-Dwur (Mountain) and Hegoldem-Dwur (Hill)

The kingdoms of **Balnorhak** and **Gilmorack** remain bastions of tradition, forged in the "Wars of Iron, Stone, and Steel." These dwarves are the guardians of the **Anvil of the Lortmil Mountains**, which yields weapons of twice the normal keenness.

- **Moradinson’s Legacy:** Characters seeking "Master Smith" or "Seeker of Artifacts" backgrounds often pursue the **Five Great Tools** detailed in Silvervein Moradinson’s _Manual of Creation_: The Brutal Pick, the Earthheart Forge, the Anvil of Songs, the Shaping Hammer, and the legendary Axe of the Dwarven Lords.

### The People’s Republic of Mordengard

In Western Oerik, the "Blessed Kingdom" ended with the revolution against **Tyrant King Hakon**, who treated his people as chattel. The transition to a **Worker's Council** created a meritocracy where status is earned via the Guilds (Soldiers, Miners, Brewers). This has birthed a culture of "revolutionary industriousness," though archival evidence suggests a lingering friction between these republicans and dwarven traditionalists from the Lortmils.

### Specialized Roles and Assets

- **The People’s Legion:** Players may adopt roles such as **Lightning Lancers** (polearms channeling mountaintop lightning) or **Udarnik** (Shock Troops).
- **Elemental Weaponry:** The Udarnik utilize the **Leadblaster**, a steel-hafted axe that fires a lead plug via bound elemental air. **Gorelka (Scorchers)** utilize "Scorch Pots" containing elemental fire.
- **Beast Integration:** Mordengarders utilize **Giant Tortoises (Gronnapel)** as mobile archery platforms and **Daggertooth Ratels** (giant badgers) for frontline combat. A character might specifically be a **Tortoise Master** or a **Ratel Handler**.

## 4. The Gnomish and Elven Enclaves

The demi-human presence in the Lortmils and the Adri Forest is defined by strategic isolationism, acting as a vital buffer against the encroaching "Undead Lords."

### High Elves and Sylvan Elves

- **High Elves of Ulek:** Known for the ancient alliance at the fortress of **Hoch Dunglorin**, these elves provide sophisticated political anchors.
- **Sylvan Elves (Sentinels of the Coldwood):** These martial protectors are locked in an eternal war against Prince Myrhal. Their specific objective is the protection of the **City of Summer Stars**, a ruined site Myrhal desperately seeks to claim.

### Rock (Flinty) Gnomes

The "Flinty Gnomes" of Avesta and the Lortmils are renowned pikemen. Archival records suggest they possess unique cultural assets:

- **The Sinking Shield:** A wooden shield that allows the wielder to sprout roots into the ground, becoming immovable by any force less than a demi-god.
- **The Orchard Club:** A +2 club that can "tap" any non-magical tree to produce a blue **Uskfruit**, prized for its sweetness and use in _potions of sweet water_.

### Peripheral Ethnicities: A Call for Field Research

Our archives are currently incomplete regarding the following groups. We classify them as **Peripheral Ethnicities** and call for further field research to better integrate them into our strategic maps:

- **Goliaths**

### The Competitive Landscape

The future of the Flanaess is currently defined by a cold war between two Undead Lords and their respective artifacts: **Prince Myrhal**, who holds the **Evil Crown of Might** in the black sprawl of Vaxhaunt, and the lich **Seragrimm**, who holds the **Good Crown of Might** in Valehurst (though he considers it blasphemous to wear). While the **Republic Dwarves of Mordengard** hold a technological edge with their elemental weaponry, the strategic balance relies on whether the **Sylvan Elves** can keep the City of Summer Stars out of Myrhal’s reach. Ultimately, the survival of the human and dwarven kingdoms depends on those who can navigate the "Low Road" and master the "Five Great Tools" of Moradinson to counter the rising necrotic tide.
